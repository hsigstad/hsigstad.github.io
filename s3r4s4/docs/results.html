Random-assignment diagnostic (April 2026)

Chi-squared test of uniform case allocation across varas within each foro-year cell (source/table/first_stage.py):

  • 225 foro-year cells tested.
  • 53% reject uniform at 5%; 45% reject at 1%.
  • Median chi² p-value: 0.027.

Takeaway. Case counts are significantly unequal across varas. Unequal volumes do not by themselves violate IV assumptions; the balance test is the more informative check. But persistent imbalance may signal systematic routing in some foros — see methods.md for follow-up work.

  • table: paper/tables/random_assignment.tex

  • On the subsample of foro-years that pass the uniform-allocation chi² test (106/494 foro-years, N = 16,723), the defendant-bank balance coefficient drops to −0.009 (t = −0.27, p = 0.79) — the imbalance disappears. First stage is somewhat weaker (F = 475) but still strong.

Sentence classifier validation (April 2026)

Hand-checked 100 random classified cases against an independent regex classifier:

  • 78% exact agreement.
  • All 22 disagreements are ACORDO ↔ EXTINTO (homologated settlements described as "julgo extinto o processo"). Both categories are excluded from the adjudicated sample, so this does not affect the IV analysis.
  • Zero misclassifications among the three adjudicated categories (PROCEDENTE / PARCIALMENTE PROCEDENTE / IMPROCEDENTE).

Unclassified cases (36,553, 10% of total): interlocutory decisions rather than final sentences — 15% tutela antecipada, 17% alternative extinction phrasing ("declaro extinto"), 10% rejected preliminary motions. Recovering the alternative-phrasing extinctions would raise the classification rate from 90% to ~92% but adds no adjudicated outcomes.

Judge rotation within varas (April 2026)

  • Median 6 judges per vara all-time; only 6% of varas have had exactly one judge.
  • Within a vara-year, one judge typically dominates:
    • Median modal-judge share within vara-year: 89%.
    • 49% of vara-years have >90% of cases decided by a single judge.
    • 86% of vara-years have >50% decided by a single judge.

Takeaway. Vara-level leniency is a reasonable approximation because the modal judge accounts for most decisions within vara-year. A weighted-judge-average construction would give a modest precision gain only in the ~14% of vara-years where the modal judge handles <50%.

Appeals (April 2026)

Linked first- and second-instance data via NPU. Of 365,667 first-instance cases:

  • 104,874 (29%) have at least one second-instance proceeding.
  • 81,758 (22%) have a formal Apelação Cível.

Appeal rate by first-instance outcome:

Outcome Appeals Total Rate
Procedente 11,800 49,881 23.7%
Parcialmente procedente 19,300 68,759 28.1%
Improcedente 27,224 71,054 38.3%
Extinto 11,905 88,580 13.4%
Acordo 2,353 50,840 4.6%

Takeaway. Losing plaintiffs appeal at 38.3% vs 26.2% for defendants when plaintiff wins. Asymmetry matters for LATE interpretation: first-instance decisions are more likely to be reversed when the plaintiff loses, attenuating the reduced-form if BCB data captures post-appeal status.
