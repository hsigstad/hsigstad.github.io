1. Brazilian credit reporting

1.1 Structure of the negative registry

Brazil's consumer credit registry is dominated by private bureaus. Serasa Experian is the largest by coverage and the one used by essentially all banks for underwriting checks. Boa Vista (SCPC), SPC Brasil (administered by CNDL, retail-oriented), and Quod (bank-consortium bureau founded 2016) are the other bureaus of note. NEEDS PRIMARY SOURCE: BCB payments/registry overview or ANBC market-share report quantifying Serasa's lead over Boa Vista/SPC/Quod.

Creditors — banks, retailers, utilities, telecoms — report delinquent accounts to the bureaus. The consumer is then recorded as negativado in the Cadastro de Inadimplentes. The creditor, not the bureau, chooses whether to list a borrower; the bureau acts as a pass-through registry and bears no independent duty to verify the underlying debt. Source: CDC.43.§1–CDC.43.§4; STJ Súmula 359.

A negative entry is consulted in essentially all formal credit applications in Brazil, and its practical effect is to shut the borrower out of bank loans, credit cards, many retail installment plans, and a range of non-credit transactions that run credit checks (rental contracts, utility accounts, business registrations with state agencies). NEEDS PRIMARY SOURCE: representative citation for the claim that a single active entry is sufficient for automated denial in bank underwriting — likely a BCB Relatório de Economia Bancária or a Febraban technical note.

1.2 Scale of negativação

As of February 2026, Serasa reports 81.7 million CPFs with at least one active negative entry, equal to 49.9% of the adult population — the highest level in the series. A decade earlier (2016) the number was approximately 59 million, a 38.1% increase over ten years. Empresarial: 8.9 million active CNPJs were negativados at end-2025, aggregating R$213 billion in reported debts. Source: Serasa Experian, Mapa da Inadimplência e Renegociação de Dívidas no Brasil, monthly release (https://www.serasa.com.br/limpa-nome-online/blog/mapa-da-inadimplencia-e-renogociacao-de-dividas-no-brasil/); press coverage in Diário do Comércio (Feb 2026) and Consumidor Moderno.

Age distribution of negativados (Serasa, 2025–2026): 41–60 years = 35.6%, 26–40 years = 33.5%, 60+ years = 19.8%, 18–25 years = 11.1%. Source: Serasa Mapa da Inadimplência, same release.

2. CDC, Cadastro Positivo, and the STJ súmulas

2.1 CDC.43 — the statutory basis

The statutory anchor for consumer rights in credit registries is CDC.43 (Lei 8.078/1990 Art. 43). Key paragraphs:

  • CDC.43.caput: Consumer right of access to information in bureaus and registries.
  • CDC.43.§1: Cadastro data must be objective, clear, true, easily understood; negative entries may not be retained beyond 5 years.
  • CDC.43.§2: "A abertura de cadastro, ficha, registro e dados pessoais e de consumo deverá ser comunicada por escrito ao consumidor, quando não solicitada por ele." This is the statutory basis for the pre-inclusion notification duty — the most load-bearing provision for 6226 litigation.
  • CDC.43.§3: Consumer may demand immediate correction of inaccurate data; bureau must notify recipients within five working days.
  • CDC.43.§4: Credit bureaus are deemed entidades de caráter público, triggering heightened consumer-protection obligations.

2.2 Súmula 359/STJ — who must notify

"Cabe ao órgão mantenedor do cadastro de proteção ao crédito a notificação do devedor antes de proceder à inscrição."

The duty of pre-inclusion notification under CDC.43.§2 belongs to the bureau (the órgão mantenedor), not to the creditor. Written notice is sufficient — proof of receipt (AR) is not required, per Súmula 404/STJ (approved 28/10/2009): "É dispensável o aviso de recebimento (AR) na carta de comunicação ao consumidor sobre a negativação de seu nome em bancos de dados e cadastros." Key precedent: REsp 783.907/RS. Failure to notify is an illicit act that gives rise to a duty to indemnify, evaluated case-by-case. Source: STJ Súmula 359, approved 13 August 2008; STJ Súmula 404, approved 28/10/2009.

STJ held in REsp 2.056.285/RS (Min. Nancy Andrighi, 25/04/2023, Informativo 773) and REsp 2.070.073 (22/09/2023) that notification exclusively by e-mail or SMS does not satisfy the pre-inclusion duty; physical correspondence is required. Caveat: REsp 2.158.450/RS (Dec 2024) may have reversed this position — verify whether the 2023 holdings still represent current STJ law before citing in the paper.

2.3 Súmula 385/STJ — moral damages and the preexisting listing

"Da anotação irregular em cadastro de proteção ao crédito, não cabe indenização por dano moral, quando preexistente legítima inscrição, ressalvado o direito ao cancelamento."

Load-bearing for our treatment definition: when an irregular inscription is added to a consumer who already has a legitimate prior listing, the consumer is still entitled to have the irregular entry cancelled, but is not entitled to moral damages. This is why the removal-order component and the moral-damages component of a 6226 sentence can move separately and why we define treatment as the removal, not the damages award.

Flexibilized by REsp 1.704.002/SP (Min. Nancy Andrighi, 11/02/2020, Informativo 665): moral damages may be awarded despite a preexisting listing if the prior listing is itself being judicially contested and the consumer demonstrates verossimilhança that the prior listing is also irregular — even before those prior lawsuits reach trânsito em julgado. Source: STJ Súmula 385, approved 27 May 2009; flexibilization in REsp 1.704.002/SP (2020).

2.4 Lei 12.414/2011 — Cadastro Positivo (distinct from the negative regime)

Lei 12.414/2011 created the positive credit registry: a separate regime recording adimplemento (on-time payments) for the purpose of building a credit history. Originally opt-in, it was converted to opt-out/automatic inclusion by LC 166/2019. The law requires that stored information be objective, clear, true, and easily understood, and entitles the consumer to access, correction, and cancellation of their positive-registry record. Source: Lei 12.414/2011, https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2011/lei/l12414.htm; amendments via LC 166/2019.

Important correction vs. earlier drafts of the paper: the pre-inclusion notification duty in negative registries does not flow from Lei 12.414/2011. It flows from CDC Art. 43 §2º and is codified in Súmula 359/STJ. Lei 12.414/2011 governs the parallel positive registry and is only tangentially relevant to the 6226 litigation. The pre-inclusion notification duty flows from CDC.43.§2, not from the Cadastro Positivo law. The draft in paper.tex:51 should be corrected accordingly.

3. Why courts: the absent administrative remedy

The institutional contrast with the United States is instructive. Under the US Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA, 15 U.S.C. §1681), credit bureaus are legally obligated to investigate disputes within 30 days, must correct or delete entries they cannot verify, and are directly liable for inaccurate reporting. The FTC and CFPB can take enforcement action, creating an administrative dispute resolution layer. Brazil does not have an equivalent process at scale.

No Brazilian institution has the power to issue a binding order compelling a credit bureau to remove a specific negative listing:

  • CDC.43.§3 gives consumers the right to demand correction, and the bureau must notify recipients within 5 working days. But the statute creates no administrative body to adjudicate whether data is inaccurate and order removal. If the creditor or bureau refuses to act, the consumer must sue.
  • Procon can convene conciliation hearings and impose administrative fines (Decreto 2.181/1997), but cannot order removal of a listing. When conciliation fails, Procon itself advises consumers to seek judicial remedies. Source: Procon-SP orientation on cadastro exclusion (https://consumidor.procon.sp.gov.br/orientacao/detalhe/164-prazos-para-exclusao-scpc-serasa).
  • SENACON (Secretaria Nacional do Consumidor) supervises credit bureaus and can fine for systemic violations, but does not adjudicate individual disputes. The consumidor.gov.br platform facilitates negotiation but outcomes are explicitly non-binding.
  • BCB operates the SCR (public credit registry for supervision) but has no regulatory authority over private bureaus (Serasa, SPC, Boa Vista). STJ has ruled BCB cannot be held liable for improper SCR entries. Source: STJ, 15/04/2021 (https://www.stj.jus.br/sites/portalp/Paginas/Comunicacao/Noticias/15042021-BC-nao-pode-ser-responsabilizado-por-inscricao-indevida-no-Sistema-de-Informacoes-de-Credito.aspx).
  • ANPD could theoretically order data deletion under LGPD, but has not established a dispute process for credit registry errors and is not a practical channel.

The practical consequence is that a wrongfully negativado consumer whose creditor refuses to remove the listing has effectively one remedy: to sue the creditor (and sometimes the bureau) in civil court, typically under the CDC, asking the judge to order the removal of the entry and — separately — to award moral-damages compensation (danos morais). This is the institutional feature our paper exploits.

The scale of judicialização reflects this: assunto 6226 reached 1,119,327 new filings nationally in 2023 (~3,747/day), making it the single most litigated consumer subject in Brazilian courts. Source: Terra / BNews reporting on CNJ data (2024); World Bank, General Principles for Credit Reporting (2011), Principle 4 (recommending effective dispute mechanisms — Brazil's system falls short).

4. Assunto 6226 — cause of action

4.1 CNJ assunto taxonomy and case numbering

Brazilian civil cases are classified by the Conselho Nacional de Justiça (CNJ) according to the Tabelas Processuais Unificadas (TPUs), established by CNJ Res. 46/2007. The TPU system defines three hierarchical classification schemes: classe (procedural type), assunto (subject matter), and movimentação (procedural acts). Tribunals must use TPU codes in their case management systems; local codes are not valid for CNJ reporting.

Assunto 6226 — *Inclusão Indevida em Cadastro de Inadimplentes* is a leaf node under the "Responsabilidade do Fornecedor" / consumer-law branch of the state-court assunto tree. The document-retention guideline attached to 6226 in the CNJ tabela is 10 years after final closure. Source: CNJ, Tabela de Assuntos da Justiça Estadual (https://www.cnj.jus.br/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/tabela-de-assuntos-justia-estadual.pdf). NEEDS PRIMARY SOURCE: pull the exact parent-node path for 6226 from the live CNJ SGT (Sistema de Gestão de Tabelas Processuais) and paste it here so the hierarchy is unambiguous in the paper.

Case numbers follow the unified format (CNJ Res. 65/2008): NNNNNNN-DD.AAAA.J.TR.OOOO where J=8 (Estadual), TR=26 (TJSP), and OOOO encodes the originating vara. The case number thus directly encodes tribunal, year, and vara — useful for parsing without reading other fields.

DataJud (CNJ Res. 331/2020) is the centralized procedural data repository that tribunals must feed monthly. Our Datajud snapshot is the source for the ~340,000 6226 cases figure at TJSP; the 118,000 classified first-instance decisions come from our own TJSP ESAJ sentence scrape.

For deeper context on CNJ, TPU, DataJud, and case management systems, see research/institutions/brazil/topics/cnj-administracao-judicial.md.

4.2 Typical fact patterns

A 6226 complaint alleges that a defendant creditor wrongfully listed the plaintiff in a consumer credit registry. The recurring fact patterns (based on our own classification of sentenças from the TJSP scrape) are:

  1. Paid debt not updated — the debt was in fact paid, but the creditor did not process the update with the bureau.
  2. Prescribed debt — the debt was extinguished by the statute of limitations but the listing persisted.
  3. Identity fraud / mistaken identity — the plaintiff never contracted with the creditor at all.
  4. Merits-contested debt — disputed fees, unauthorized charges, services never rendered.
  5. Missing pre-inclusion notice — the creditor or the bureau failed to honor CDC.43.§2 (violates Súmula 359).

Relief almost always combines an obrigação de fazer ordering removal of the entry with a monetary award for danos morais, the size of which is set at the judge's discretion.

4.3 Volume and plaintiff-win rate

Between 2013 and 2024 the Tribunal de Justiça de São Paulo (TJSP) alone produced approximately 365,000 first-instance sentences on 6226 cases (source: our own sentenças scrape). The breakdown: EXTINTO 24%, IMPROCEDENTE 19%, PARCIALMENTE PROCEDENTE 19%, ACORDO 14%, PROCEDENTE 14%, unclassified 10%. Among adjudicated cases (PROCEDENTE + PARCIALMENTE PROCEDENTE + IMPROCEDENTE), the plaintiff wins in approximately 63% of rulings. Source: own computation from source/table/descriptives.py.

The 80% figure should not be read mechanically as the share of listings that were "wrongful": a plaintiff win can reflect (a) creditor non-appearance (revelia), (b) procedural violations (missing Súmula 359 notification), (c) the inversão do ônus da prova in favor of the consumer under CDC.6.VIII, or (d) a merits adjudication that the underlying debt was nonexistent. The decomposition is an empirical task for §2.3/§6 of the paper. 5. TJSP civil procedure and random assignment

5.1 TJSP geography: foros and varas

TJSP is organized into foros — first-instance court units that correspond roughly to municipalities, or, in large cities, to neighborhoods within a municipality (the foros regionais of the capital). Each foro contains one or more varas cíveis, which are the first-instance civil chambers presided over by individual judges. Source: LC Estadual 1.336/2018, Art. 1 (current organic law: https://www.al.sp.gov.br/repositorio/legislacao/lei.complementar/2018/lei.complementar-1336-28.12.2018.html); LC 980/2005 was in force for most of the sample period (2013–2018).

5.2 Random distribution of cases to varas (sorteio eletrônico)

Within a foro, new cases are assigned to varas by a computerized distribution system. The general regime distinguishes two types of distribution: (i) sorteio — free distribution among the varas competent for the classe and assunto; and (ii) dependência — obligatory redirection to the vara already holding a related case, which requires indication of the referenced process and the legal ground. Under sorteio the system implements a stratified round-robin that balances caseload across varas and is, conditional on foro and filing date, as good as random with respect to the identity of the plaintiff and the facts of the case. Source: TJSP, Normas de Serviço da Corregedoria-Geral de Justiça (NSCGJ), distribution rules at arts. 1209–1219 and 1279–1280-A; TJSP Orientações Público Interno — Distribuidor (https://www.tjsp.jus.br/PrimeiraInstancia/OrientacaoPublicoInterno/Distribuidor). The same random-assignment feature has been exploited in Araújo, Ferreira, Lagaras, Moraes, Ponticelli & Tsoutsoura (2023, JFE) on bankruptcy cases at TJSP. They explicitly state and test the random-assignment assumption across varas within foros, providing a balance test showing no significant differences in case characteristics across pro-debtor vs. pro-creditor courts. This is the closest methodological precedent for our design. Note: Lichand & Soares (2014, J. Law & Econ.) study courts and entrepreneurship in Brazil but use the geographic rollout of juizados especiais, not within-foro random assignment. They should be cited for the research question, not for the identification strategy.

5.3 Case flow for a 6226 case

A 6226 case proceeds through: filing (CPC.319, petição inicial) → initial judicial review → defendant summons and response (CPC.335, contestação, 15-day deadline) → (optional) conciliation hearing under CPC Art. 334 (Lei 13.105/2015) → evidentiary phase or julgamento antecipado (CPC.355) → first-instance judgment (sentença, CPC.485/CPC.487). The first-instance decision classifies the case as:

  • Procedente — plaintiff wins in full.
  • Parcialmente procedente — plaintiff wins in part (typically with a reduced moral-damages award but still an order to remove the entry).
  • Improcedente — plaintiff loses on the merits.
  • Extinto sem resolução de mérito — dismissed on procedural grounds (CPC.485).
  • Acordo (homologado) — settlement, judicially ratified.

For the purposes of the paper we define a plaintiff win as procedente or parcialmente procedente, because in both cases the court orders the removal of the contested entry.

Enforceability despite appeal. By default, appeal (apelação) has suspensive effect (CPC.1012). However, in 6226 cases judges routinely grant tutela antecipada / tutela provisória ordering removal of the negative listing early in the case (CPC.300, tutela de urgência; CPC.497, obrigação de fazer). When the sentença confirms this tutela provisória, CPC.1012.§1.V creates an exception to the suspensive effect: the removal order takes immediate effect despite appeal. This is why we treat the first-instance outcome as the relevant treatment — the removal order is typically operative well before the appeal is decided.
