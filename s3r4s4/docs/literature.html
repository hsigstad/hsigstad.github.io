    Literature

    References drawn from the March 11, 2026 project presentation are listed under "Core references" below. See docs/briefs/presentation_march2026.md for how each fits into the argument. Additional entries come from the literature search run on 2026-04-14.

    Core references (presentation)

    • Chemin (2009) — Journal of Public Economics 93(1-2):114-125.

      • topic: impact of the judiciary on entrepreneurship; evaluation of Pakistan's access-to-justice programme.
      • relevance: existing evidence that court access fosters entrepreneurship in LMICs; companion result to Lichand-Soares.
      • file: chemin2009impact.pdf

    • Dobbie, Goldsmith-Pinkham, Mahoney, Song (2020) — Journal of Finance 75(5):2377-2419.

      • topic: credit and labor-market consequences of bad credit reports ("Bad Credit, No Problem?").
      • relevance: closest prior on how negative credit records affect borrowing; we extend to entrepreneurship and the judicial-redress margin.
      • file: dobbie2020bad.pdf

    • Dobbie, Song (2015) — American Economic Review 105(3):1272-1311.

      • topic: debt relief and debtor outcomes under US Chapter 13 bankruptcy protection.
      • relevance: shows debtor protection affects real outcomes; motivates interest in the de-blacklisting margin.
      • file: dobbie2015debt.pdf

    • Herkenhoff, Phillips, Cohen-Cole (2024) — Review of Economic Studies 91(5):2832-2877.

      • topic: how credit constraints affect job finding, sorting, and aggregate output.
      • relevance: theoretical/quant link from credit records to labor reallocation for unemployed workers.
      • file: herkenhoff2024how.pdf

    • Herkenhoff, Phillips, Cohen-Cole (2021) "The Impact of Consumer Credit Access on Self-Employment and Entrepreneurship" — Journal of Financial Economics 141(1):345-371.

      • topic: links TransUnion credit reports to Census admin employment + Schedule C + LBD; exploits bankruptcy flag removal; stock of self-employment unchanged but gross flows rise; entrants borrow 12% more and sort into capital-intensive industries; 200% higher rate of employer-firm ownership.
      • relevance: defines the outcome set for our entrepreneurship results (gross flows, entry into employer firms, borrowing at entry). The stock/flow distinction is especially important — we should track gross flows, not just levels.
      • file: herkenhoff2021impact.pdf

    • Lichand, Soares (2014) — Journal of Law and Economics 57(2):459-499.

      • topic: access to justice and entrepreneurship via Brazil's juizados especiais civis.
      • relevance: prior judge-design paper in Brazilian civil courts; source for "random assignment by computerized algorithm" identification idea.
      • file: lichand2014access.pdf

    • Ponticelli, Alencar (2016) — QJE 131(3):1365-1413.

      • topic: court enforcement, bank loans, and firm investment after Brazil's 2005 bankruptcy reform.
      • relevance: shows judicial efficiency matters for credit supply and firm outcomes in Brazil; Alencar is a coauthor on this project.
      • file: ponticelli2016court.pdf

    Contribution framing

    Per the deck: the literature is silent on how absence of consumer protection interacts with credit reports to affect entrepreneurship. This project occupies that gap by exploiting judicial de-blacklisting decisions as a source of variation in whether wrongful Serasa listings are corrected.

    Credit reports and negative information removal

    • Liberman, Neilson, Opazo, Zimmerman (2018) "The Equilibrium Effects of Information Deletion: Evidence from Consumer Credit Markets" NBER WP 25097.

      • topic: 2012 Chilean reform forced credit bureaus to delete defaults for 2.8M individuals; studies individual and equilibrium effects on credit supply.
      • relevance: directly comparable Latin American setting — our case-level mechanism is the micro analogue of their macro experiment.
      • file: liberman2018equilibrium.pdf

    • Bos, Nakamura (2014) "Should Defaults Be Forgotten? Evidence from Variation in Removal of Negative Consumer Credit Information" FRB Philadelphia WP.

      • topic: Swedish quasi-experiment in retention time for negative credit information.
      • relevance: welfare-level motivation for why removing a Serasa flag matters.
      • file: bos2014should.pdf

    • Musto (2004) "What Happens When Information Leaves a Market? Evidence from Post-Bankruptcy Consumers" Journal of Business.

      • topic: US credit market responses when bankruptcy flags are removed from credit reports.
      • relevance: early evidence that flag removal raises credit access and (some) default risk.
      • file: musto2004what.pdf

    • Dobbie, Keys, Mahoney (2017) "Credit Market Consequences of Credit Flag Removals".

      • topic: follow-up evidence on credit flag removals and borrowing outcomes.
      • relevance: reinforces the flag-removal mechanism central to our identification.
      • file: dobbie2017credit.pdf

    • Garmaise, Natividad (2017) "Consumer Default, Credit Reporting, and Borrowing Constraints" Journal of Finance.

      • topic: effect of reported default on subsequent borrowing using Peruvian microfinance data.
      • relevance: developing-country evidence on how a single negative flag shapes access.
      • file: garmaise2017consumer.pdf

    • Elul, Gottardi (2015) "Bankruptcy: Is It Enough to Forgive or Must We Also Forget?" AEJ: Micro.

      • topic: theoretical model of default retention time in a repeated moral hazard / adverse selection setting.
      • relevance: theory benchmark for welfare consequences of cleaning credit histories.
      • file: elul2015bankruptcy.pdf

    Information sharing and credit registries

    • Pagano, Jappelli (1993) "Information Sharing in Credit Markets" Journal of Finance.

      • topic: foundational theory of why lenders voluntarily share information.
      • relevance: framework for thinking about what negative-information removal does to pooling equilibria.
      • file: pagano1993information.pdf

    • Jappelli, Pagano (2002) "Information Sharing, Lending and Defaults: Cross-Country Evidence" Journal of Banking & Finance.

      • topic: cross-country evidence that credit info sharing reduces defaults and raises lending.
      • relevance: macro evidence on the credit-registry institution.
      • file: jappelli2002information.pdf

    • Djankov, McLiesh, Shleifer (2007) "Private Credit in 129 Countries" Journal of Financial Economics.

      • topic: cross-country comparison of creditor rights and information sharing as drivers of private credit.
      • relevance: Brazil sits in the cluster of countries where private registries matter a lot.
      • file: djankov2007private.pdf

    • Brown, Zehnder (2009) "The Emergence of Information Sharing in Credit Markets" Journal of Financial Intermediation.

      • topic: experimental evidence on the emergence and effects of credit information sharing.
      • relevance: mechanism evidence on why reporting matters for borrower discipline.
      • file: brown2009emergence.pdf

    • Sutherland (2018) "Does Credit Reporting Lead to a Decline in Relationship Lending?" Journal of Accounting and Economics.

      • topic: staggered entry of lenders into a US commercial credit bureau reshapes who lends to whom.
      • relevance: reporting changes the margin of new credit access — relevant for interpreting entry of new lenders after a Serasa removal.
      • file: sutherland2018does.pdf

    • Martinez Peria, Singh (2014) "The Impact of Credit Information Sharing Reforms on Firm Financing" World Bank PRWP 7013.

      • topic: firm-level effects of credit bureau reforms across 63 countries.
      • relevance: cross-country benchmark on firm financing effects of registry reforms.
      • file: martinezperia2014impact.pdf

    Courts, judicial enforcement, and credit/firm outcomes

    • Jappelli, Pagano, Bianco (2005) "Courts and Banks: Effects of Judicial Enforcement on Credit Markets" Journal of Money, Credit, and Banking.

      • topic: Italian cross-province variation in court efficiency and credit supply.
      • relevance: canonical paper linking courts to credit access.
      • file: jappelli2005courts.pdf

    • Lilienfeld-Toal, Mookherjee, Visaria (2012) "The Distributive Impact of Reforms in Credit Enforcement: Evidence from Indian Debt Recovery Tribunals" Econometrica.

      • topic: Indian DRT creation accelerated enforcement with distributive consequences across borrowers.
      • relevance: closest evidence that court-level reforms change who gets credit.
      • file: lilienfeldtoal2012distributive.pdf

    • Visaria (2009) "Legal Reform and Loan Repayment: The Microeconomic Impact of Debt Recovery Tribunals in India" AEJ: Applied.

      • topic: individual-loan-level evidence from DRT rollout.
      • relevance: methods template — court reform shocks linked to admin credit data.
      • file: visaria2009legal.pdf

    • Giacomelli, Menon (2017) "Does Weak Contract Enforcement Affect Firm Size? Evidence from the Neighbour's Court" Journal of Economic Geography.

      • topic: spatial variation in Italian court efficiency and firm size.
      • relevance: evidence that court performance has real effects on firms.
      • file: giacomelli2017does.pdf

    • González-Torres, Rodano (2020) "Court Efficiency and Aggregate Productivity: The Credit Channel" Bank of Italy WP.

      • topic: court efficiency and aggregate productivity through credit supply.
      • relevance: macro framing of the credit channel from courts.
      • file: gonzaleztorres2020court.pdf

    • Ponticelli (2011) "Firms, Local Courts and the New Bankruptcy Law in Brazil" (working-paper precursor to Ponticelli-Alencar 2016).

      • topic: initial evidence on firm investment responses to the 2005 Brazilian bankruptcy reform.
      • relevance: earlier iteration of the core Brazilian benchmark; useful for identification discussion.
      • file: ponticelli2011firms.pdf

    • Araújo, Ferreira, Lagaras, Moraes, Ponticelli, Tsoutsoura (2021) "The Labor Effects of Judicial Bias in Bankruptcy" NBER WP 28640.

      • topic: uses judge-level variation in Brazilian bankruptcy courts to estimate labor market effects.
      • relevance: methodologically the closest Brazilian paper — same identification strategy (judge leniency inside Brazilian courts) applied to firm labor outcomes.
      • file: araujo2021labor.pdf

    • Pinheiro, Cabral (1999) "Credit Markets in Brazil: The Role of Judicial Enforcement and Other Institutions" IADB Research Network WP.

      • topic: institutional description of Brazilian courts and credit.
      • relevance: background on the Brazilian setting.
      • file: pinheiro1999credit.pdf

    Judge/examiner-design methodology

    • Aizer, Doyle (2015) "Juvenile Incarceration, Human Capital, and Future Crime: Evidence from Randomly Assigned Judges" QJE.

      • topic: judge leniency IV for juvenile incarceration effects.
      • relevance: canonical modern implementation of the design.
      • file: aizer2015juvenile.pdf

    • Bhuller, Dahl, Løken, Mogstad (2020) "Incarceration, Recidivism, and Employment" Journal of Political Economy.

      • topic: Norwegian judges as instruments; admin-data linkage like ours.
      • relevance: benchmark for combining judge-leniency IV with administrative outcomes.
      • file: bhuller2020incarceration.pdf

    • Dobbie, Goldin, Yang (2018) "The Effects of Pre-Trial Detention on Conviction, Future Crime, and Employment: Evidence from Randomly Assigned Judges" AER.

      • topic: bail judge leniency IV.
      • relevance: template for the civil-case analogue.
      • file: dobbie2018effects.pdf

    • Maestas, Mullen, Strand (2013) "Does Disability Insurance Receipt Discourage Work? Using Examiner Assignment to Estimate Causal Effects of SSDI Receipt" AER.

      • topic: examiner-assignment IV in a civil setting.
      • relevance: non-criminal use of examiner IV.
      • file: maestas2013does.pdf

    • Frandsen, Lefgren, Leslie (2023) "Judging Judge Fixed Effects" AER.

      • topic: tests for the identifying assumptions of judge-IV designs.
      • relevance: robustness tests we will need to run (monotonicity, exclusion).
      • file: frandsen2023judging.pdf

    • Chyn, Frandsen, Leslie (2024) "Examiner and Judge Designs in Economics: A Practitioner's Guide" NBER WP 32348.

      • topic: practitioner's guide for examiner/judge designs.
      • relevance: reference for LOO construction, monotonicity tests, standard errors.
      • file: chyn2024examiner.pdf

    • Dobbie, Liberman, Paravisini, Pathania (2021) "Measuring Bias in Consumer Lending" Review of Economic Studies.

      • topic: marginal-outcome tests for discrimination in lending using quasi-random loan officers.
      • relevance: LOO-style design in a civil/financial setting.
      • file: dobbie2021measuring.pdf

    • Arnold, Dobbie, Yang (2018) "Racial Bias in Bail Decisions" QJE.

      • topic: leniency-based tests of discrimination in bail.
      • relevance: heterogeneity tests across judges.
      • file: arnold2018racial.pdf

    Bankruptcy, fresh start, and entrepreneurship

    • Livshits, MacGee, Tertilt (2007) "Consumer Bankruptcy: A Fresh Start" AER.

      • topic: quantitative model of US consumer bankruptcy trading off insurance against credit costs.
      • relevance: theory framework for interpreting how removing bad information affects entrepreneurship.
      • file: livshits2007consumer.pdf

    • Ayotte (2007) "Bankruptcy and Entrepreneurship: The Value of a Fresh Start" JLEO.

      • topic: theory of how bankruptcy protection affects entrepreneurial risk-taking.
      • relevance: theory framing.
      • file: ayotte2007bankruptcy.pdf

    • Fossen (2014) "Personal Bankruptcy Law, Wealth, and Entrepreneurship: Theory and Evidence from the Introduction of a Fresh Start".

      • topic: German fresh-start reform and entry into self-employment.
      • relevance: closest quasi-experimental evidence linking personal debt relief to entrepreneurship.
      • file: fossen2014personal.pdf

    • Mankart, Rodano (2015) "Personal Bankruptcy Law, Debt Portfolios, and Entrepreneurship".

      • topic: quantitative model with secured and unsecured debt and occupational choice.
      • relevance: structural framing for our reduced-form estimates.
      • file: mankart2015personal.pdf

    • Dobbie, Song (2020) "Targeted Debt Relief and the Origins of Financial Distress: Experimental Evidence from Distressed Credit Card Borrowers" AER.

      • topic: randomized debt relief for distressed borrowers.
      • relevance: complements the court-mediated debt relief channel.
      • file: dobbie2020targeted.pdf

    • Celentani, García-Posada, Gómez-Pomar (2023) "Fresh Start Policies and Small Business Activity: Evidence from a Natural Experiment".

      • topic: Spanish personal-insolvency reform and small business outcomes.
      • relevance: European evidence on the same question.
      • file: celentani2023fresh.pdf
