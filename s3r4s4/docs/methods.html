Methods

Methodology, specifications, and design choices. Numerical findings (first-stage F, balance coefficients, classifier agreement rates, appeal rates, etc.) live in results.md.

Judge leniency IV

Identification strategy

  • Exploit random assignment of SERASA-listing civil cases to varas (not judges) via computerized algorithm (cf. Lichand-Soares 2014). The unit of randomization is the vara; judge identity enters only through which judge happens to occupy the vara at the time of filing.
  • In most foros, one judge operates per vara, so vara-leniency and judge-leniency coincide for those cases. Where a vara has seen multiple judges over time, vara-leniency is constructed as a weighted average of the leniencies of the judges who have served in it.
  • Use leave-one-out vara leniency (win-rate) as instrument for court outcomes.
  • Restrict sample to foros/comarcas with more than one vara in the filing year, so that within-foro variation exists.

Estimand

  • Parameter of interest: effect of being removed from the Serasa blacklist (plaintiff "wins" case) on post-filing credit access, firm activity, and entrepreneurship.
  • Interpretation: LATE for marginal cases whose outcome is shifted by variation in judge leniency.

Control group

  • Plaintiffs who filed a SERASA-listing lawsuit in the same year but lost. Window −5 to +5 years around the filing year.

Key assumptions

  • Random assignment of cases to varas within foro-year.
  • Exclusion restriction: judge leniency affects downstream outcomes only through the court decision.
  • Monotonicity: more lenient judges weakly increase plaintiff-win probability for all cases.

Specification (pre-BCB, following the March 2026 deck)

$$Active_j = \alpha_j + \alpha_{dc} + \alpha_{ct} + \beta_S^t \cdot Post_Processo_{jt} \times WonCase_{jc} + \varepsilon_{jt}$$

Fixed effects:

  • firm $\alpha_j$ (cnpj_basico)
  • comarca × case year $\alpha_{dc}$
  • calendar year × case year $\alpha_{ct}$

Standard errors clustered at the firm level.

  • won_case = 1 if sentenca in {PROCEDENTE, PARCIALMENTE PROCEDENTE}.
  • bias_magistrado (in Ramon's current code): leave-one-out judge win rate computed among cases the judge decides in the same comarca/year cell. Caveat: the correct randomization unit is the vara, not the judge; the judge-level instrument is a reasonable approximation when one judge runs each vara (see results.md §Judge rotation for the modal-judge share).
  • Sample: year_processo <= 2019 (so the full ±5 window is observable).
  • Event-study variant: ib5.id_dist_processo ## c.won_case with t = −1 omitted; analogous IV implementation interacts bias_magistrado with each event-time dummy and instruments the corresponding won_case × event-time interactions.

Outcomes (reported in the March 2026 deck)

Pre-BCB stage (CNPJ/Receita side only):

  • active_firm: 1 if firm cadastral status is active in a given calendar year.
  • n_plants, new_firms, closed_firms: firm-establishment dynamics.
  • mei, simples: tax-regime indicators derived from simples_data.

BCB stage (from Ramon's 5_rodandoregressoes.do, run inside the Central Bank):

  • Credit stock: foc_vl_a_vencer, foc_vl_vencido, foc_vl_prejuizo, foc_vl_limite_credito.
  • Pricing: foc_pc_tax_eft (effective interest rate).
  • Guarantees: foc_qt_gar_fidej, foc_qt_gar_nao_fidej.
  • Each outcome in three transforms: levels, log(x+1), bin = 1[x > 0].
  • Each outcome separately on CPF and CNPJ panels, and as tot_* = sum of both sides (see 4_pareamento.do).

Balance and random-assignment diagnostics

Balance test. Regress each of four pre-filing covariates (defendant type dummies, log valor da ação, filing month) on LOO vara leniency conditional on foro × year FE. Joint F-test on all four. Implementation in source/table/first_stage.py; estimates in results.md §Balance table.

The test found defendant type not balanced. Possible explanations:

  1. Vara specialization within foro. Some foros may route bank cases to specific varas (e.g., consumer vs general cível). If routing depends on defendant type rather than plaintiff type, random assignment of plaintiffs is maintained but the exclusion restriction requires conditioning on defendant type.
  2. Mechanical relationship. Banks may be harder defendants (bigger legal teams, lower settlement), so varas that happen to draw more bank cases show lower win rates. This is a reflection problem — a genuine outcome effect rather than a selection violation — but it means the balance test is not purely a randomization check.

Action items:

  • Check whether specific foros route bank cases to dedicated varas (e.g., "Vara de Fazenda Pública" vs "Vara Cível"). If so, restrict the sample to varas that hear all defendant types, or control for defendant type.
  • Re-run first stage and balance with defendant-type controls.
  • Consider clustering SE at the vara level instead of foro × year, since vara is the unit of randomization.

Random-assignment diagnostic. Chi-squared test of uniform case allocation across varas within each foro-year cell. See results.md §Random-assignment diagnostic for rejection rates.

Unequal case volumes do not by themselves violate IV assumptions; what matters is whether case characteristics are non-randomly sorted across varas, which the balance test addresses. But if volume imbalance reflects systematic routing (e.g., specialized varas handling a subset of case types), the "random assignment within foro" assumption may not hold in all foros.

Action items:

  • Restrict to foros where the chi² test does not reject uniform, and check whether first-stage and balance results are stable.
  • Investigate the largest-volume foros (Foro Central Cível, Santo Amaro, São José do Rio Preto) to confirm that all varas in the sample hear 6226 cases and that redistribution events do not create systematic sorting.

Sentence classifier validation

Protocol. Hand-check 100 random classified cases against an independent regex classifier. Report exact-agreement rate, the nature of disagreements (which categories get confused), and misclassification rate among adjudicated categories specifically.

Classifier. In source/clean/serasa.py, uses ordered regexes:

  1. Primary: julgo\s(procedente( em parte)?|improcedente|parcialmente procedente) — catches explicit merits rulings.
  2. Fallback: julgo.{0,20}extint[ao]|homologo|extingo — catches procedural closures and homologated settlements.

See results.md §Sentence classifier validation for the agreement rate and a breakdown of unclassified cases.

Judge rotation within varas

Measurement. For each (vara, filing year), compute the share of 6226 decisions attributable to the modal judge, plus the count of distinct judges observed within the vara over the full sample and within each vara-year. See results.md §Judge rotation for distributional summaries.

Party identifiers for BCB linkage

The consulta processual data (parte_1a.csv) contains plaintiff names and a sequential parte_id, but no CPF or CNPJ. The TJSP consulta does not expose personal identifiers on its public-facing interface. Linkage to BCB administrative records will need to rely on one of:

  1. Plaintiff full name + foro (approximate matching), or
  2. CPF extraction from the sentence text (some sentences mention the plaintiff's CPF in the body), or
  3. Datajud API (may contain masked CPF in some fields), or
  4. A dedicated name-matching step inside BCB by Leonardo/Ramon.

This is a constraint for the BCB-side pipeline — flagged for Ramon.

Appeals linkage

Method. Link first- and second-instance data via NPU (the same case number in both instances). Compute appeal rate by first-instance outcome and decompose asymmetry between losing plaintiffs and losing defendants. See results.md §Appeals for rates and the LATE-interpretation implications.

Open implementation questions

  • Instrument construction at the vara level. The current bias_magistrado is a judge-level LOO. Randomization is at the vara, so the principled instrument is vara-level leniency. Candidates:

    1. Pure vara leniency: LOO win-rate over all cases decided in the vara.
    2. Weighted-judge-average: weighted mean of the leniencies of all judges who have served in the vara, weights proportional to each judge's total decisions (possibly across all case types, to gain power).
    3. Hybrid: weighted-judge-average when the vara has seen multiple judges; pure judge LOO when only one judge has ever served.

    Option 2 is likely the most powerful because it leverages each judge's full decision history for leniency estimation, not just sentences within this case type.

  • The control-group choice ("filed in same year but lost") does not use sentenca in {IMPROCEDENTE} cleanly — need to confirm handling of PARCIALMENTE PROCEDENTE cases (counted as wins in Ramon's code).

  • Whether year_processo <= 2019 restriction should be relaxed for outcomes measured at shorter horizons.

  • Whether to cluster at vara level rather than firm level given the IV source of variation.
