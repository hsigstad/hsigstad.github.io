    Operação Faroeste (TJ-BA, Bahia)

    Overview

    The largest judicial corruption operation in Brazilian history, targeting a scheme of buying and selling judicial decisions at the Tribunal de Justiça da Bahia (TJ-BA). The scheme centered on land disputes in the extreme western region of Bahia near the border with Piauí — an area of enormous agricultural value (Formosa do Rio Preto is the 8th-largest agricultural municipality in Brazil). The disputed area initially covered approximately 366,000 hectares (five times the size of Salvador) and was later found to extend to nearly 800,000 hectares.

    Time period: Mid-2010s through 2020+ First phase: November 19, 2019 Courts: TJ-BA (primary), STJ (jurisdiction over desembargadores) Relator: Minister Og Fernandes (STJ)

    Structure of the scheme

    The MPF identified three nuclei:

    1. Judicial nucleus: Desembargadores, juízes, and servidores who issued favorable decisions
    2. Legal/intermediary nucleus: Lawyers who brokered between magistrates and rural producers
    3. Economic nucleus: Rural producers and agribusiness figures who paid for favorable rulings

    Key acts: injunctions to open land registrations (matrículas), cancellation of competing registrations, and subdivision of disputed lands. A crucial non-judicial figure was Adailton Maturino, who posed as honorary consul of Guinea-Bissau.

    Minister Og Fernandes noted competing factions within the TJ-BA — "true wings that were somewhat antagonistic to each other" — but both operated with the same modus operandi. One group's decisions were overturned by the other, not out of legal conscience, but because rival groups were vying for illicit advantages.

    Pattern: Family as essential link

    The Faroeste case illustrates a systematic pattern:

    1. Professional cover: Children practiced law, giving legitimate reasons for meetings and handling money
    2. Trust: Family bonds eliminated need for external intermediaries in the final delivery
    3. Deniability: Payments could be framed as legitimate legal fees
    4. Asset concealment: Relatives purchased assets in their own names
    5. Cross-family networks: Children of different desembargadores collaborated (Arthur Barata + Ivanilton Junior)
    6. Obstruction capacity: Family warned magistrates and helped destroy evidence

    Legal outcomes

    • 6+ desembargadores became réus at STJ
    • 3+ juízes became réus
    • 5+ family members became réus (Vasco, Karla, Márcio, Rui, Arthur)
    • CNJ aposentadoria compulsória for at least one desembargadora
    • First delação premiada by a desembargadora in Brazilian history (subsequently rescinded)
