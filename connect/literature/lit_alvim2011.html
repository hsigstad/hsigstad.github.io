    Alvim (2011) -- Operação Hurricane: um juiz no olho do furacão

    Account by Desembargador J. E. Carreira Alvim of Operação Hurricane (TRF-2, Rio de Janeiro, 2007). Note: this is the defendant's self-serving account claiming innocence. Only chapters 1-6 were read (~10 chapters total).

    Key facts about the operation

    • Operação Hurricane was a 2007 Federal Police operation targeting corruption at the Tribunal Regional Federal da 2a Região (TRF-2) in Rio de Janeiro, connected to bingo/gambling enterprises.
    • Authorized by STF minister Cezar Peluso; search and arrest warrants executed against 26 individuals including desembargadores.
    • Alvim was arrested along with his son-in-law and Desembargador Ricardo Regueira. Rede Globo broadcast the arrests as breaking news.
    • The accusation centered on decisions favorable to bingo companies (Betec, Reel Token, Abraplay) -- Alvim claims he merely ordered return of seized machines pending expert examination, consistent with other desembargadores' existing injunctions.
    • Key piece of evidence: a wiretapped phrase "a minha parte em dinheiro, tá?" ("my share in cash, ok?") -- Alvim claims this was edited/spliced from a conversation about sponsoring an academic conference in Buenos Aires, later supported by expert analysis from Prof. Ricardo Molina (Unicamp).

    Family connections documented

    • Silvério Júnior: Alvim's son-in-law (married to daughter Luciana), arrested alongside him. Silvério Júnior is the son of Desembargador Silvério Cabral, who sat on the same court (TRF-2, Segunda Turma) where Alvim served. A direct two-generation family network within one appellate court.
    • Alvim's sister Maria Helena: a federal judge in Minas Gerais.
    • Alvim's father: a "vitorioso advogado" (successful lawyer) and politician in Minas Gerais.
    • The wiretap of Silvério Júnior was justified by the Ministério Público Federal explicitly because he was married to Alvim's daughter -- the family tie was the stated basis for the investigation.

    The procurador's key quote on family ties

    A procurador da República stated in an interview that ideally the wiretapping should have been extended to "outros desembargadores do mesmo Tribunal, que tinham filhos, genros, mulheres e outros parentes advogados" (other desembargadores of the same court who had children, sons-in-law, wives, and other relatives who were lawyers). The operation was rushed because of the upcoming court presidency election.

    Alvim's own claim about the prevalence of family ties

    "a maioria dos desembargadores deste país tem um filho, ou filha, ou genro, ou nora, ou mulher, ou companheira e até 'namorada' advogando no foro."

    Translation: "the majority of desembargadores in this country have a son, or daughter, or son-in-law, or daughter-in-law, or wife, or partner, or even a girlfriend practicing law in the courts."

    This is a defendant's self-serving argument (if everyone does it, I'm not special), but it is consistent with the empirical patterns in the connect project's data.

    Institutional insights

    Court presidency politics and patronage

    • The presidency of TRF-2 was traditionally determined by seniority (antiguidade), but could be overridden by secret ballot with the word "preferencialmente" (preferentially) in the internal rules.
    • Alvim claims the operation was timed to prevent him from becoming president.
    • Cargos de confiança (trust positions) in the court are maintained as "verdadeiros feudos" (true fiefdoms) through agreements between incoming and outgoing presidents, in the personal interest of desembargadores who "patrocinam a permanência no cargo de seus apadrinhados" (sponsor the permanence of their protégés).
    • Secret balloting enables betrayal: desembargadores promised votes to Alvim's face but voted otherwise under secrecy.

    Chilling effect

    • After the operation, one unnamed desembargador who had previously issued a pro-bingo decision revoked his own ruling sua sponte (without any party requesting it) the moment he learned of Alvim's arrest, and later took early retirement out of fear.
    • Other desembargadores became "literally cowed" (literalmente acuados), afraid to issue injunctions against the government.

    Oversight and wiretapping

    • Wiretaps lasted 335 days (legal limit: 15 days, renewable once for 15 more).
    • Ambient listening device installed in the ceiling of Alvim's vice-presidency office, with the knowledge of the court president (Desembargador Frederico Gueiros).
    • Some desembargadores were described as being "da estrita confiança da Polícia Federal e do Ministério Público Federal" (in the strict confidence of the Federal Police and the MPF) -- i.e., informants within the court.

    Elite social networks

    • Alvim's career illustrates how elite legal networks form: he befriended future STF minister Carlos Velloso as a law student at UFMG; as procurador da República in Brasília he socialized with future ministers of superior courts; his wife hosted gatherings attended by "as maiores autoridades da República."
    • Friendship with STF minister Ellen Gracie (who later voted against him in a habeas corpus, casting the tie-breaking vote twice).

    Relevance to the connect project

    1. Family networks within courts are empirically pervasive: Both the prosecution and the defense agree that desembargadores routinely have relatives practicing as lawyers. The disagreement is only about whether this constitutes corruption.
    2. Specific family structure: The Alvim-Silvério nexus shows a two-generation connection (desembargador's son becomes lawyer, marries another desembargador's daughter) within a single court -- exactly the kind of connection the project measures.
    3. Cargos de confiança as feudos: Court trust positions are allocated through patronage networks tied to the court presidency, reinforcing the family/friendship infrastructure.
    4. The investigation itself was triggered by family ties: The wiretap on Silvério Júnior was justified by his marriage to Alvim's daughter, showing that authorities themselves view family connections as a red flag for corruption.
    5. Chilling effect: The operation demonstrates that anti-corruption enforcement can itself distort judicial behavior, relevant for interpreting any time-series variation around enforcement events.
