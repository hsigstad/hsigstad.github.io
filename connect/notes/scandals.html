Judicial Corruption Scandals: Detailed Summaries

Judicial Corruption Scandals: Detailed Summaries

Summaries of major judicial corruption episodes in Brazil, with emphasis on the role of family members of judges. Based on ~600 news articles collected from Folha, O Globo, Piauí, Estadão, Conjur, Migalhas, JOTA, Agência Pública, The Intercept, Crusoé, BBC, and regional outlets. See references/news/episodes_catalog.csv for the full episode list and references/news/episodes.csv for article-episode mappings.

Operação Faroeste (TJ-BA, Bahia)

Overview

The largest judicial corruption operation in Brazilian history, targeting a scheme of buying and selling judicial decisions at the Tribunal de Justiça da Bahia (TJ-BA). The scheme centered on land disputes in the extreme western region of Bahia near the border with Piauí — an area of enormous agricultural value (Formosa do Rio Preto is the 8th-largest agricultural municipality in Brazil). The disputed area initially covered approximately 366,000 hectares (five times the size of Salvador) and was later found to extend to nearly 800,000 hectares.

Time period: Mid-2010s through 2020+ First phase: November 19, 2019 Courts: TJ-BA (primary), STJ (jurisdiction over desembargadores) Relator: Minister Og Fernandes (STJ)

Structure of the scheme

The MPF identified three nuclei:

  1. Judicial nucleus: Desembargadores, juízes, and servidores who issued favorable decisions
  2. Legal/intermediary nucleus: Lawyers who brokered between magistrates and rural producers
  3. Economic nucleus: Rural producers and agribusiness figures who paid for favorable rulings

Key acts: injunctions to open land registrations (matrículas), cancellation of competing registrations, and subdivision of disputed lands. A crucial non-judicial figure was Adailton Maturino, who posed as honorary consul of Guinea-Bissau.

Minister Og Fernandes noted competing factions within the TJ-BA — "true wings that were somewhat antagonistic to each other" — but both operated with the same modus operandi. One group's decisions were overturned by the other, not out of legal conscience, but because rival groups were vying for illicit advantages.

Family connections

1. Desembargadora Sandra Inês Rusciolelli → Son Vasco Rusciolelli

  • Relative: Vasco Rusciolelli Azevedo (son), lawyer
  • Role: Direct intermediary for receiving bribes. In a monitored sting (5th phase, March 2020), the payment chain worked: rural producer Nelson Vigolo → lawyer Vanderlei Chilante → lawyer Julio Cesar Cavalcanti (traveled from Rondonópolis to Salvador) → Vasco, who received cash at a motel in Salvador
  • After receiving the money, Vasco passed the bag to his girlfriend inside a university campus to create distance, then the money was taken to his mother's residence
  • Seizures: PF found R$208,800 inside a Jaguar, R$15,000 with a funcionário leaving the garage, R$35,000 in the desembargadora's bedroom
  • Amounts: R$4 million negotiated across cases, R$2.4 million actually received
  • Mechanism: Son's legal profession gave cover for meeting lawyers; family trust eliminated need for external intermediaries in final payment leg
  • Intercepted conversations: Vasco and lawyer Julio Cesar discussed wanting to kill assessor Antonio Roque Neves; Julio said he already had Roque's address, CPF, and license plate
  • Outcome: Both entered Brazil's first delação premiada by a desembargadora (2021), naming 68 people across 39 annexes (12 desembargadores, 12 juízes, 15 lawyers, 16 TJ-BA employees). Delação rescinded in November 2024 — they stopped attending hearings, refused to pay fines, refused to sell a R$4.5M property in Praia do Forte. Sandra admitted she only agreed to help her son.

2. Desembargadora Maria da Graça Osório Pimentel → Niece Karla Janaína Leal

  • Relative: Karla Janaína Leal (niece), lawyer
  • Role: Conduit for bribe money. Maintained "lifestyle incompatible with income." Created an association of servidores who worked for her aunt, which prosecution identified as money laundering mechanism
  • Mechanism: Aunt paid Karla's personal bills from corruption proceeds; the association channeled funds through judicial staff
  • Outcome: Both charged with organização criminosa and lavagem de dinheiro

3. Desembargadora Maria do Socorro Barreto Santiago → Son-in-law Márcio Miranda

  • Relative: Márcio Duarte Miranda (son-in-law/genro), lawyer
  • Role: Part of scheme. Evidence of Socorro's lavish acquisitions laundered through others: R$9,000 artwork with receipt in domestic employee's name; jewelry and artwork found in undisclosed residence
  • Outcome: Both became réus; Maria do Socorro arrested and removed from TJ-BA presidency

4. Desembargadora Lígia Maria Ramos Cunha Lima → Sons Rui Barata and Arthur Barata

  • Relatives: Arthur Gabriel Ramos Barata Lima (son, lawyer) — primary intermediary; Rui Barata Lima (son, lawyer, known as "Rui Barata Filho") — operator
  • Role of Arthur: Negotiated sale of mother's decisions directly. According to delator Julio Cesar:
    • Negotiated a decision for R$400,000 — R$100,000 to Julio as intermediary, R$300,000 delivered to Arthur in front of a car dealership in Salvador
    • One day after his mother signed the favorable decision (September 4, 2018), Arthur purchased a Ford Ranger pickup for R$145,000 at the same dealership
    • Also negotiated with Ivanilton da Silva Junior (son of another desembargador) for decisions in cases involving fazendeiro Nei Castelli
  • Specific cases: Four judicial proceedings plus influence in a fifth; two involving Equatorial Transmissora S.A. power line disputes in Correntina
  • Total received: R$950,000 across identified cases
  • Rui Barata Filho: Targeted by PF in July 2024; identified by delatores as operator of sentence sales. Also the focus of Operação Patronos — PF targets R$35M in illegal advantages; his patrimônio grew 4.56x after his mother became desembargadora
  • Outcome: STJ Corte Especial received denunciation against Lígia and both sons (February 2025) for organização criminosa and lavagem de dinheiro. Lígia also accused of destroying evidence and intimidating servidores after the operation began

5. Desembargador Ivanilton da Silva → Son Ivanilton Junior

  • Relative: Ivanilton da Silva Junior (son)
  • Role: Worked with Arthur Barata to negotiate sale of decisions — creating a cross-family network where sons of different desembargadores collaborated as brokers
  • Mechanism: The sons' professional overlap and personal relationships facilitated coordination across chambers

Pattern: Family as essential link

The Faroeste case illustrates a systematic pattern:

  1. Professional cover: Children practiced law, giving legitimate reasons for meetings and handling money
  2. Trust: Family bonds eliminated need for external intermediaries in the final delivery
  3. Deniability: Payments could be framed as legitimate legal fees
  4. Asset concealment: Relatives purchased assets in their own names
  5. Cross-family networks: Children of different desembargadores collaborated (Arthur Barata + Ivanilton Junior)
  6. Obstruction capacity: Family warned magistrates and helped destroy evidence

Legal outcomes

  • 6+ desembargadores became réus at STJ
  • 3+ juízes became réus
  • 5+ family members became réus (Vasco, Karla, Márcio, Rui, Arthur)
  • CNJ aposentadoria compulsória for at least one desembargadora
  • First delação premiada by a desembargadora in Brazilian history (subsequently rescinded)

Operação Última Ratio (TJ-MS, Mato Grosso do Sul)

Overview

Launched October 24, 2024, by the PF with Receita Federal support. Targeted the TJ-MS for systematic venda de sentenças, with the defining feature being the "esquema cruzado" (cross-scheme) where children of desembargadores served as intermediaries for each other's parents.

Courts: TJ-MS (Campo Grande), also TCE-MS Time period: At least 2014–2024 Scope: 44 search warrants in MS, DF, SP, MT; ~200 federal agents Predecessor operations: Operação Mineração de Ouro (2021, TCE-MS) and Operação Lama Asfáltica

The "Esquema Cruzado"

CNJ Corregedor Mauro Campbell described the core finding:

"Foi revelada a existência de um verdadeiro consórcio entre desembargadores do TJ-MS e seus filhos advogados, cujos interesses eram inegavelmente prestigiados, muitas vezes, de maneira cruzada, mediante o recebimento indireto de vantagens indevidas."

The mechanism: Desembargador A's children gained favorable rulings before Desembargador B, while B's children gained favorable rulings before A. Designed to avoid the most obvious conflict of interest while ensuring mutual benefit. Children's law firms served as financial conduits.

Intercepted conversation between court staff: "Do Sideni também tem e... só que sempre pelos filhos, sabe? Sempre pelos filhos." Another: "Aí não sei como o CNJ não pega, a Polícia Federal não pega."

Desembargadores afastados

  2. Sideni Soncini Pimentel — president-elect (4ª Câmara Cível)
  3. Vladimir Abreu da Silva — vice-president-elect
  4. Marcos José de Brito Rodrigues
  5. Alexandre Aguiar Bastos

Also targeted: Julio Roberto Siqueira Cardoso (aposentado; R$2.7M cash found at home) and Divoncir Schreiner Maran (aposentado).

Operação Sisamnes (STJ)

Overview

Named after Sisamnes, the Persian judge skinned alive for taking bribes. The investigation was triggered by the assassination of lawyer Roberto Zampieri in Cuiabá (December 5, 2023) and the subsequent analysis of his phone (9,000+ WhatsApp messages). Now under STF Minister Cristiano Zanin's relatoria.

Court: STJ (Superior Tribunal de Justiça) Time period: At least 2019–2024 Phases: 9+ phases from November 2024 through June 2025

How the scheme worked

Step 1 — Access to draft decisions: Corrupt assessors and chiefs of staff inside STJ minister offices extracted draft decisions before publication. In some cases, the server would create, save, print, and delete the document within minutes.

Step 2 — Brokering by lobbyists: Andreson Gonçalves and the late Roberto Zampieri received drafts and shared them with interested parties. If favorable, they "sold" it; if unfavorable, they offered to change it — for a price.

Step 3 — Payment: Cash, gold bars, Patek Philippe watch (~US$75,000), shell company transfers via Florais Transportes (Andreson's firm, flagged by Coaf for R$2.8 billion in suspicious transactions), inclusion of relatives in lucrative contracts.

Step 4 — Front: Andreson's wife, advogada Mirian Ribeiro, was formally contracted by interested parties, lending legitimacy. Sometimes the contracts were real legal work, sometimes pure fronts.

Corrupt assessors

Servidores in 11 of 33 STJ gabinetes were touched by the investigation:

  • Márcio José Toledo Pinto — served in gabinetes of Ministers Isabel Gallotti and Nancy Andrighi. His shell company Marvan received R$4M from Florais over 2.5 years. Created, saved, printed, and deleted draft decisions within minutes. Demitted September 2024
  • Daimler Alberto de Campos — chief of staff to Minister Isabel Gallotti for over a decade. Bank account received R$8.8M (2019–2023). Continued receiving salary up to R$35,500/month while investigated
  • Rodrigo Falcão de Oliveira Andrade — chief of staff to Minister Og Fernandes. Minister made 82 transfers totaling R$900K to Falcão (Og says for delegated personal bills). Police found 3 empty Rolex boxes with R$266K in receipts
  • Waleska Bertolini Vieira Mussalem — gabinete of Minister Moura Ribeiro. On the same day she drafted a decision, Andreson messaged he had just spoken "com a Valesca"

The lobbyist couple: Andreson Gonçalves and Mirian Ribeiro

  • Andreson used a fraudulently obtained OAB card (annulled since 2020), owned four aircraft, ran Florais Transportes (R$2.8B in suspicious transactions flagged by Coaf). Arrested November 2024, released to house arrest, re-arrested after continuing to operate from home
  • Mirian Ribeiro (wife, lawyer): Of 201 cases at STJ, 47 passed through Minister Moura Ribeiro's gabinete with 72.5% win rate (vs 43.5% elsewhere). In 16 cases, contracted after case assigned to Moura Ribeiro. Handled 13 J&F/JBS cases; in June 2023, JBS transferred R$16.2M to her office

Family members of judges/ministers

Direct family involvement in corruption

  • Mauro Thadeu de Moraes (son of TJ-MT desembargador Sebastião de Moraes Filho): inserted into a R$12 million legal services contract as payment for a favorable decision by his father. In messages: "acertar as honrárias"
  • Anna Carolina Noronha (daughter of STJ Minister João Otávio de Noronha): received R$300,000 from Afare I (Fource group fund linked to the scheme) between 2020–2021. Father claims legitimate legal fees
  • Catarina Buzzi (daughter of STJ Minister Marco Buzzi): rented office from Haroldo Filho (key Fource figure), had contacts with Zampieri. An empresário claimed to have paid R$1.1 million in her favor for a case in her father's gabinete. Did not have OAB card at the time
  • Karine Nunes Marques (sister of STF Minister Kássio Nunes Marques): contracted by Haroldo Filho for a Petrópolis brewery case at STJ. Andreson warned: "Haroldo tem que ver que contratar parentes de ministro do Supremo não é certeza de ganhar"
  • Maria Carolina Feitosa de Albuquerque Tarelho (stepdaughter of STF Minister Gilmar Mendes): reportedly contracted for Petrópolis cases
  • Aline Gonçalves de Sousa (wife of TRF-1 desembargador César Jatahy): received R$938,000 from Florais Transportes

Broader "filhotismo" pattern

  • 2024 investigation: half of STJ ministers have children/relatives advocating in ~1,000 pending cases
  • Relatives called "príncipes" inside STJ
  • 2022 law (signed by Bolsonaro) worsened the problem by allowing legal consultation "verbally or in writing, at the discretion of the lawyer and client, without requiring a formal mandate" — enabling relatives to work invisibly

Operação Mais Valia (TRT-1, Rio de Janeiro)

Overview

Launched March 2, 2021, targeting the TRT-1 (labor court). Offshoot of Operação Tris in Idem (which suspended Governor Witzel). Triggered by delação of ex-Health Secretary Edmar Santos. Handled by STJ Minister Nancy Andrighi.

Court: TRT-1 (Tribunal Regional do Trabalho, 1ª Região, Rio de Janeiro) Time period: At least 2018–2020 Charges: Corruption, money laundering, criminal organization

Desembargadores involved

  1. Marcos Pinto da Cruz — identified as principal leader; offered bribes to Governor Witzel
  2. José da Fonseca Martins Junior — ex-president TRT-1 (2019–2021)
  3. Fernando Antonio Zorzenon da Silva — ex-president TRT-1 (2017–2019)
  4. Antonio Carlos de Azevedo Rodrigues

Two additional judges (Epilógio Baptista, Múcio Borges) forcibly retired in February 2022.

Family members as operators

Each desembargador had a designated family member collecting bribes:

Desembargador Family operator Relationship
Marcos Pinto da Cruz Eduarda Pinto da Cruz Sister
José da Fonseca Martins Jr. Sônia Regina Dias Martins Wife
Fernando Zorzenon da Silva Marcello Cavanellas Zorzenon Son
Antonio Carlos Rodrigues Leila Maria Gregory Cavalcante Wife

The mechanism

  1. Targets: Companies with outstanding labor debts — bus consortia (Transcarioca, Santa Cruz, Intersul, Internorte) and health-sector Organizações Sociais (Pró-Saúde), plus companies like Atrio Service and MPE Engenharia
  2. Coercion to hire designated firms: Companies pressured to retain specific law firms — particularly Eduarda Pinto da Cruz's firm
  3. Payments as legal fees: Companies paid what appeared to be honorários advocatícios; fees funneled to desembargadores through family operators
  4. Favorable decisions in return: Inclusion in Plano Especial de Execução (special labor debt execution plan), acceleration of labor cases, COVID-era suspension of payment obligations → allowed OSs to obtain certidões negativas needed for government contracts
  5. State government connection: Marcos promised to kick back 20% of OS inclusion values to Witzel and Edmar Santos

Amounts

  • Marcos received R$3.6 million from sister Eduarda (2018–2020)
  • Eduarda paid R$1.3 million to lawyer Pedro D'Alcântara Miranda Neto
  • 3 desembargadores condemned to up to 20 years for R$6.8M in bribes (R$13.5M negotiated)
  • While afastados since 2021, earned R$4.7M in salaries without working
  • CNJ aposentou compulsoriamente (2025)

Harm to workers

The scheme directly harmed workers: the Plano Especial was designed to help companies pay labor debts to workers. By selling inclusion and granting COVID suspensions, desembargadores allowed companies to delay/avoid paying workers while collecting bribes from the same system.

TJ-MT — Venda de Sentenças and the Zampieri Assassination

Overview

The origin story of the Sisamnes investigation. On December 5, 2023, lawyer Roberto Zampieri was shot 12 times in his car in Cuiabá. His phone revealed the "true marketplace of judicial decisions" at both TJ-MT and STJ.

The assassination

  • Hitman: Antônio Gomes da Silva, posing as a priest, paid R$40,000
  • Organizer: Coronel Caçadini, ran hit squad "Comando C4" with price list: R$100K for deputies, R$150K for senators, R$250K for ministers
  • Mandante: Aníbal Manoel Laurindo, landowner who suspected Zampieri of using judicial influence to steal a R$100M rural property

TJ-MT desembargadores and family connections

Sebastião de Moraes Filho and João Ferreira Filho (both 1ª Câmara Cível de Direito Privado, handling land disputes):

  • Accepted bribes in cash, gold bars, and luxury watches (Patek Philippe ~US$75,000)
  • Son Mauro Thadeu de Moraes inserted into R$12M contract as payment for father's decision — described in messages as "acertar as honrárias"
  • CNJ also found R$200,000 paid to "o filho do velho" (the old man's son) for a ruling
  • Both afastados by CNJ in August 2024

Predecessor: Operação Asafe (2010)

Earlier TJ-MT scandal featured extensive family connections:

  • Célia Cury — wife of desembargador José Tadeu Cury, practicing lawyer
  • Cláudio Manoel Camargo Junior — son-in-law of José Tadeu Cury, lawyer
  • Rodrigo Vieira — another son-in-law, lawyer
  • Phellipe Oscar Rabello Jacob — son of Eduardo Jacob (lawyer and suspended TRE judge)
  • Multiple desembargadores suspended: Evandro Stabile, José Luiz de Carvalho, Carlos Alberto da Rocha, Donato Ojeda

Cross-Scandal Patterns

The family intermediary model

Across all scandals, family members serve the same functions:

  1. Professional cover: Legal practice justifies meetings, cash handling, large transfers
  2. Trust: Eliminates need for external intermediaries in final payment leg
  3. Deniability: Payments framed as legitimate honorários
  4. Asset concealment: Assets in relatives' names harder to trace
  5. Cross-family networks: Children of different judges collaborate (TJ-MS esquema cruzado, TJ-BA Arthur Barata + Ivanilton Junior)
  6. Obstruction: Family can destroy evidence and warn magistrates

The "esquema cruzado" as institutional adaptation

The most sophisticated form appears in TJ-MS: judges' children don't practice before their own parent (which would trigger impedimento), but before each other's parents. This is an explicit adaptation to circumvent recusal rules while maintaining reciprocal benefit. The physical colocation of offices (same building) facilitated coordination.

Recusal rules as inadequate

None of these scandals involve violations of formal impedimento rules (which cover direct relatives as lawyers). The corruption operates through:

  • Children practicing before other judges in the same court
  • Children receiving payments as "legal fees" for work formally unrelated to the parent's decisions
  • Lobbyists and intermediaries creating additional layers of separation
  • Shell companies and fractionated cash transactions

This is precisely what our paper's empirical evidence quantifies: the favoritism that exists beyond the margin of recusal compliance.

Source: Vasconcelos (2005), Juízes no banco dos réus. See docs/notes/lit_vasconcelos2005.md.

One of Brazil's earliest major judicial corruption operations. Judges Rocha Mattos, Casem Mazloum, and Ali Mazloum charged with selling sentences and judicial information to benefit criminals, politicians, and businessmen.

Family connections:

  • Sister Vera Cecília Vieira de Moraes: Ex-auditor at the Receita Federal
  • Niece Vera Cristina Vieira de Moraes: PF delegate suspected of helping the scheme

Operação Hurricane (TRF-2, Rio de Janeiro, 2007)

Source: Alvim (2011), Operação Hurricane: um juiz no olho do furacão. See docs/notes/lit_alvim2011.md. Note: Alvim is the defendant and claims innocence — account is self-serving.

Targeted desembargadores at TRF-2 for corruption linked to gambling/bingo operations.

Family connections:

  • Son-in-law Silvério Cabral Júnior: Arrested alongside desembargador Alvim. Silvério Jr. was the son of another desembargador (Silvério Cabral) at the same court — creating a two-generation family network

Also connected to Paulo Medina (STJ) — first STJ minister aposentado compulsoriamente by CNJ (2010), for corruption related to slot machine companies.

Operação Habeas Pater (TRF-1, Brasília, 2023)

Family connections:

  • Son Ravik de Barros Bello Ribeiro (lawyer): arrested with father desembargador Cândido Ribeiro (ex-president of TRF-1). Father and son suspected of selling habeas corpus decisions to drug traffickers from Operação Flight Level 2. R$270K cash found at son's home. 17 search warrants in Brasília, BH, and São Luís. The operation name — "Habeas Pater" — itself references the father-son relationship.
