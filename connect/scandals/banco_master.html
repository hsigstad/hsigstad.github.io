Vorcaro’s Web at the STF

How the owner of Brazil’s largest bank fraud cultivated financial relationships with Supreme Court families

Not bribery — but alleged conflicts of interest
Unlike the other scandals on this site, Banco Master is not (yet) a criminal operation against sitting judges. The relationships documented here involve contracts, consultancies, investments, and payments between Daniel Vorcaro / Banco Master and family members of STF ministers. No criminal charges have been filed against the ministers themselves. But the pattern is extraordinary: the owner of a bank under investigation cultivated financial ties with relatives of the very court that judges cases related to his bank and his liberty.
Daniel Vorcaro, owner of Banco Master, cultivated financial relationships with the families of at least four STF ministers — through law firm contracts, resort investments, consultancies, and payments routed through intermediary companies.

12 relatives of 8 STF ministers practice law at the Supreme Court

The Banco Master case is not an isolated phenomenon. At least 12 relatives of 8 STF ministers actively practice law at the Supreme Court, creating a permanent web of potential conflicts of interest:

Why this matters for our research
The Banco Master scandal illustrates a mechanism distinct from the direct bribery schemes documented in other operations: structural conflicts of interest through family financial relationships. Ministers do not need to be personally corrupt for justice to be compromised. When a minister’s wife holds a R$129M contract with a bank, or a minister’s brothers sell a company to the bank owner’s brother-in-law for R$35M, the independence of judicial decisions on cases involving that bank becomes untenable — regardless of the minister’s subjective intentions. This is precisely what our paper quantifies: the causal effect of family connections on case outcomes.

Why It Matters

What this scandal reveals about family connections at the Supreme Court

A different kind of capture
The Banco Master scandal reveals a form of institutional capture that operates through legal channels rather than criminal ones. Law firm contracts, resort investments, and consultancy fees are all nominally legitimate transactions. But when they flow from a bank under investigation to the families of the judges who decide that bank’s fate, the line between legitimate business and conflict of interest dissolves — and with it, public confidence in the independence of the Supreme Court.
The pattern across scandals
Across every scandal documented on this site — from the esquema cruzado at TJ-MS to the marketplace of decisions at the STJ to Banco Master at the STF — the mechanism is the same: family members serve as the bridge between money and judicial power.
  • At TJ-MS: Children of judges practiced before each other’s parents
  • At the STJ: Lobbyists paid judges through intermediaries and family connections
  • At the STF: A bank owner cultivated financial ties with ministers’ spouses, brothers, and children
The scale escalates. The sophistication increases. But the fundamental vulnerability remains: the family connection.
