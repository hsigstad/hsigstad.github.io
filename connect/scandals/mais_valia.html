The Designated Family Operators

Each desembargador had a specific family member acting as their bribe collector

Offshoot of Operação Tris in Idem — which suspended Governor Wilson Witzel — triggered by the delação premiada of ex-Health Secretary Edmar Santos. Handled by STJ Minister Nancy Andrighi.
— Origin of the investigation
The structural signature of Mais Valia: each desembargador had a designated family member (sister, wife, or son) serving as their personal bribe collector. Companies with labor debts paid "legal fees" that flowed upward through family operators to judges.
How it worked
Companies with outstanding labor debts were pressured to retain specific law firms — particularly Eduarda Pinto da Cruz's firm. Payments were disguised as honorários advocatícios (legal fees), then funneled from the family operators to the judges. In return, judges granted inclusion in the Plano Especial de Execução, accelerated labor cases, and exploited COVID-era suspensions of payment obligations — allowing Organizações Sociais to obtain certidões negativas needed for government contracts.

The Money Machine

A closed loop of coercion, bribery, and favorable decisions

The corruption cycle: companies with labor debts were coerced into hiring designated firms, whose "legal fees" flowed through family operators to judges, who returned the favor with beneficial rulings — creating a self-reinforcing machine. Marcos additionally kicked back 20% of OS inclusion values to Governor Witzel and Health Secretary Edmar Santos.

The Harm to Workers

How the scheme directly delayed payments owed to workers

The Plano Especial de Execução was designed to help companies pay their labor debts to workers. By selling inclusion and granting COVID-era suspensions, the desembargadores allowed companies to delay or avoid paying workers — while collecting bribes from the very system meant to protect them.
Direct harm to workers
Unlike many judicial corruption schemes where victims are abstract, the Mais Valia scheme directly harmed workers awaiting labor debt payments. The Plano Especial was their mechanism for recovery. By selling access and suspending obligations, judges converted a worker-protection instrument into a bribe-extraction machine — workers waited longer or never received what they were owed.

The family operator pattern
The clean symmetry — sister, wife, son, wife — is the structural signature of this scandal. Each desembargador relied on a single designated family member to handle the money. This mirrors what our paper quantifies: family members serving as intermediaries between the judiciary and external parties, exploiting trust and kinship to insulate corrupt transactions.

Why It Matters

What this scandal reveals about family intermediaries in the judiciary

The designated family operator model
Mais Valia demonstrates the purest form of the family intermediary pattern: each judge had exactly one designated family member handling money. The relationship types — sister, wife, son, wife — show how different kinship ties all serve the same structural function. This is precisely what our paper's empirical evidence quantifies: family connections as channels for influence between judges and external parties.
The pattern
The Mais Valia case illustrates the labor court variant of judicial corruption through family connections:
  • Designated operators: Each judge had exactly one family member collecting bribes
  • Professional cover: Payments disguised as legal fees (honorários)
  • Institutional capture: Two consecutive TRT-1 presidents were scheme members
  • COVID exploitation: Pandemic measures weaponized against workers
  • Worker harm: Bribes extracted from the very system designed to protect workers
  • State connections: Kickbacks extended to the governor's office
