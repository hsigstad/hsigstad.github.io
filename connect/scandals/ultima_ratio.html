The "Esquema Cruzado"

How judges' children practiced before each other's parents to evade conflict-of-interest rules

Foi revelada a existência de um verdadeiro consórcio entre desembargadores do TJ-MS e seus filhos advogados, cujos interesses eram inegavelmente prestigiados, muitas vezes, de maneira cruzada, mediante o recebimento indireto de vantagens indevidas.
— CNJ Corregedor Mauro Campbell
The "esquema cruzado": Judge A's children gain favorable rulings from Judge B, and vice versa — circumventing the most obvious conflict-of-interest rules while maintaining reciprocal benefit.
How it worked
Formal impedimento rules only apply when a lawyer-relative appears directly before their own parent. The cross-scheme exploits this gap: Sideni's children practiced before Vladimir, and Vladimir's children practiced before Sideni. No formal recusal was triggered, yet both families benefited. The physical co-location of their offices — in the same building — facilitated coordination.

Connections Beyond TJ-MS

How this scandal links to corruption at TJ-MT and the STJ

Lobbyist Andreson Gonçalves connected the TJ-MS scheme to TJ-MT and STJ corruption. The assassination of lawyer Roberto Zampieri in Cuiabá revealed a "marketplace of judicial decisions" spanning multiple courts.
The Zampieri phone
The assassination of lawyer Roberto Zampieri in Cuiabá (December 5, 2023) was the triggering event for the Sisamnes investigation. His phone contained 9,000+ WhatsApp messages about buying and selling judicial decisions — revealing that the corruption extended far beyond Mato Grosso do Sul to the STJ and 14 tribunals across Brazil.

Why It Matters

What this scandal reveals about family connections in the judiciary

The gap in recusal rules
None of the families in the esquema cruzado violated formal impedimento rules. The children avoided practicing directly before their own parents. The corruption operates through the gap between formal recusal compliance and actual favoritism — precisely what our paper's empirical evidence quantifies.
The pattern
The Última Ratio case distills the family intermediary model to its purest form:
  • Professional cover: Legal practice justifies meetings and cash handling
  • Trust: Family bonds eliminate need for external intermediaries
  • Deniability: Payments framed as legitimate legal fees
  • Institutional adaptation: Cross-scheme design explicitly circumvents recusal rules
  • Physical infrastructure: Shared office building facilitates coordination
