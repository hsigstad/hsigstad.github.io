Operação Faroeste

The largest judicial corruption operation in Brazilian history — a sprawling scheme of buying and selling judicial decisions at TJ-BA over land disputes in extreme western Bahia, where ~800,000 hectares of prime agricultural land were carved up through forged registrations, purchased injunctions, and family-run bribe networks.

The Three Nuclei

How the MPF described the criminal organization behind Faroeste

The MPF identified three interlocking nuclei: magistrates who issued decisions, lawyers (often family members) who brokered, and rural producers who paid. Competing factions within TJ-BA each ran the same modus operandi.
Adailton Maturino dos Santos
A key intermediary who posed as the honorary consul of Guinea-Bissau, Maturino brokered land registrations through corrupt judges. His title gave him a veneer of diplomatic legitimacy while orchestrating land grabs across extreme western Bahia.

The Payment Chain

How cash physically traveled from a rural producer to a desembargadora's bedroom

The cinematic payment chain: cash traveled from a rural producer in Bahia through two lawyer intermediaries (one traveling from Rondonópolis, MT), to the desembargadora's son at a motel, then via his girlfriend at a university campus to create distance, and finally to the mother's residence.

The rescinded delação
In 2021, Sandra Rusciolelli and her son Vasco became the first desembargadora to enter a delação premiada, naming 68 people across 39 annexes: 12 desembargadores, 12 juízes, 15 lawyers, and 16 TJ-BA employees. However, the delação was rescinded in November 2024, casting doubt on the use of this testimony in ongoing prosecutions.

Why It Matters

What Faroeste reveals about family connections in the judiciary

Family members as the critical infrastructure
In every major family identified in Faroeste, the relative served as the physical conduit for bribe money: Vasco received cash at a motel, Karla ran a shell association, Arthur negotiated prices at a car dealership, Ivanilton Junior collaborated across families. The family relationship provided trust, access, and deniability — the same channels our paper's empirical evidence quantifies.
The pattern across five families
Faroeste demonstrates the family intermediary model at industrial scale:
  • Physical cash handling: Sons and nieces physically carried bags of money
  • Trust: Only family members handled the most sensitive link in the chain
  • Layered deniability: Multiple handoffs (motel → girlfriend → campus → residence)
  • Cross-family collaboration: Arthur and Ivanilton Jr brokered for each other's parents
  • Professional cover: All intermediaries were practicing lawyers
  • Competing factions: Even rival groups within TJ-BA used the same family-based model
