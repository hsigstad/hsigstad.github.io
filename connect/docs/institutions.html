    Brazilian Labor Courts (Justica do Trabalho)

    <!-- slug: labor-courts -->

    Overview

    • Federal-level judiciary branch specializing in labor relations
    • Three-tier structure: Superior Labor Court (TST) > Regional Labor Courts (TRTs, 24 total) > Court units (varas trabalhistas)

    Structure

    • Each court (foro trabalhista) divided into one or more court units (varas)
    • Each court unit has one chief judge (juiz titular) and one substitute judge (juiz substituto)

    Case assignment

    • Cases randomly distributed to court units
    • Exception: cases connected to a past case (same litigants, same issue) assigned to same court unit (dependencia/prevencao)
    • Data includes indicator for random vs non-random assignment

    Recusal rules (impedimento)

    • Judge must recuse if lawyer of any party is up to a third-degree relative (includes in-laws, excludes cousins)
    • Party must allege recusal; judge then rules on request
    • If recused: case goes to substitute judge in same court unit, or next court unit if no substitute
    • Legal basis: Articles 144 and 146 of Codigo de Processo Civil (2016), CNJ resolution (2015)

    Recusal compliance (empirical)

    • Direct relatives: judges recuse in nearly all cases (41-42pp drop in main judge deciding)
    • About half of recusals go to substitute judge in same court unit (potential for continued favoritism)
    • Colleagues of relatives: judges recuse in only about half of cases (29pp pre-2015, 16pp post-2015)
    • 2015 CPC reform did not noticeably increase compliance for colleague connections
    • Cousins (not covered by rules): no change in judge assignment

    Case types

    <!-- slug: case-types -->

    • Top subjects: overtime work (38%), prior notice of dismissal (28%)
    • Outcomes: plaintiff (partial) win (38%), settlement (34%)
    • Median duration: 214 days filing to decision
    • 97% decided within data coverage period

    Recusal rules — detailed legal basis

    <!-- slug: recusal-rules -->

    • Pre-2015: impedimento only when lawyer actively acting in the case (not just a member of the firm)
    • 2015 CNJ Resolution (March 2, 2015): extended to cases where relative is socio, associado, colaborador, or empregado of the law firm, or maintains any professional link even if sporadic
    • NCPC Article 144, III, S3 (2016): formalized this — impedimento extends to members of the same law firm "even if they do not directly intervene in the case"
    • Impedimento is materia de ordem publica — judge can recognize it ex officio
    • Suspensao (suspicion of bias) is not materia de ordem publica — must be raised by a party
    • In practice: the other side may not want to raise impedimento due to repeated interaction with the judge

    Contingency fees and lawyer incentives

    <!-- slug: contingency-fees -->

    • Plaintiff lawyers: typically receive 20-30% contingency fee (share of value awarded)
    • Defendant lawyers: typically on fixed wage contracts independent of case outcome
    • This asymmetry potentially explains why favoritism effects are found only for plaintiff-side connections

    Legal dynasties — quantitative context

    <!-- slug: legal-dynasties -->

    • Judges: dynastic bias ~101x (1.5% probability if parent is judge vs 0.015% otherwise)
    • Lawyers: dynastic bias ~6x (23% if parent is lawyer vs 4%)
    • Comparable to mayors (96x) and state legislators (920x)
    • 52% of active magistrates report having relatives in private legal practice (Sadek 2006)
    • In top 6 ranked law firms: 29/455 partners and 126/6,255 employees are relatives of judges
    • Partners in top law firms 2x more likely to be relatives; employees 5x more likely; children of judges 8x more likely

    Impedimento violations and workarounds

    <!-- slug: impedimento-workarounds -->

    • Scheme 1: Hire a colleague of a relative of the judge (not formally covered until 2015)
    • Scheme 2: Hire a relative of the judge in a different case (quid pro quo)
    • Scheme 3: Relative acts as partner/associate in law firm but does not sign petitions — acts as lobbyist in corridors ("parentes-lobistas")
    • CNJ and corregedorias have "very timid" enforcement (Almeida 2014)
    • Three ex-corregedores at STJ caught voting on cases where their children were lawyers — blamed system failures

    Constitutional and statutory basis [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    • TST (Tribunal Superior do Trabalho): LOMAN.12.
    • TRTs (24 Regional Labor Courts): LOMAN.13.
    • Substantive labour law: CLT (Consolidação das Leis do Trabalho, DL 5.452/1943).
    • Procedure: CLT (CLT.763 onward) supplemented by CPC (Lei 13.105/2015, effective 18 Mar 2016).
    • Judicial guarantees (CF.95): vitaliciedade, inamovibilidade, irredutibilidade — protect independence.
    • Advocacia privada (CF.133): advocate is "indispensável à administração da justiça".

    Anti-manipulation of assignment (CPC.144.§2) [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    • Parties may not engineer impediment by substituting counsel after case distribution.
    • Why this matters for the design: identification relies on no-manipulation of assignment conditional on the randomisation strata. This rule is the institutional backstop that makes the no-manipulation assumption credible.

    Broader impedimento formalization (CPC.144.§3) [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    • Companion to the existing "Recusal rules — detailed legal basis" section above.
    • The 2016 CPC formalized in CPC.144.§3 that impedimento extends to members of the same law firm, "even if they do not directly intervene in the case". Converts the 2015 CNJ resolution into statutory form.
    • Suspeição (CPC.145): subjective grounds (friendship/enmity, gifts, financial interest). Not matéria de ordem pública — must be raised by a party. Connect's treatment focuses on impedimento, not suspeição.
    • CNJ Resolução 200/2015: the specific CNJ act referenced in the existing section. Extends impedimento to indirect kinship via hidden arrangements.

    Hearing procedure (CLT.846, CLT.849, CLT.850) [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    • CLT prescribes an audiência una (single unified hearing) — conciliation attempt, defense, evidence, and judgment in one session. Most courts fractionate into 2–3 sessions in practice.
    • Audiência inaugural / de conciliação: mandatory conciliation proposal (CLT.846). If both parties agree, settlement is homologated and the case ends.
    • Audiência de instrução e julgamento: evidence + razões finais + renewed conciliation proposal (CLT.850).
    • If interrupted, continuation must be scheduled within 30 days (CLT.849).
    • Why this matters: the "judges schedule first hearings ~20% earlier for connected plaintiff lawyers" finding (summary.md) operates on this scheduling step. The hearing date is a judge-controlled margin and a pre-ruling favouritism channel.

    Settlement observability — a methodological advantage [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    • Nearly all labor-court settlements are judicially mediated and observable in the data — unusual internationally.
    • Pre-2017: out-of-court settlements were unenforceable against indisposable rights, pushing virtually all settlement activity into the mandatory conciliation hearing.
    • Post-2017 (CLT.855-B–CLT.855-E, added by Lei 13.467/2017): formal procedure for judicial homologation of extrajudicial agreements, requiring separate attorneys on each side and judicial review. Even these settlements appear in records.
    • Contrast with civil courts (CPC.784): private agreements there are directly enforceable as título executivo extrajudicial; judicial homologation optional. Civil settlements are largely unobservable.
    • Implication for connect: settlement (acordo) rates and amounts are well-measured outcomes. Differential settlement on connected cases is a meaningful margin of favouritism.

    Sample-period reform: Lei 13.467/2017 [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    Effective 11 November 2017 — introduces filing costs and honorários de sucumbência. The project's 2014–2018 sample window straddles this reform.

    • Custas processuais (CLT.789, CLT.790): 2% of claim value, charged to the losing party.
    • Honorários de sucumbência (CLT.791-A): 5–15% of judgment value, assessed against the losing party on each claim.
    • *Justiça gratuita* threshold tightened (CLT.790.§3): automatic eligibility only for those earning ≤40% of RGPS ceiling; above that, must affirmatively prove insufficiency.
    • No-show penalty (CLT.844.§2): if plaintiff misses the hearing, case is archived and plaintiff owes costs to refile, even as a justiça gratuita beneficiary.

    Filing volumes around the reform (CNJ / TST data):

    Year New 1st-instance cases Change
    2016 ~2.7M (peak) —
    2017 ~2.6M (Nov spike ~207k in one month) −3%
    2018 ~1.7M −34%
    • November 2017 spike: monthly filings nearly doubled as workers rushed to file under the old regime.
    • Immediate collapse in 2018, sustained through 2019.
    • Composition change: speculative/low-probability claims (pedidos aventureiros) appear to have dropped disproportionately.
    • ADI5766 (October 2021) later struck down key provisions — but outside the project's sample window.

    Implication for connect: the 2018 sample is not comparable to 2014–2016 in composition. Any analysis mixing pre- and post-reform years should either control for filing-year or split the sample at November 2017.

    Conciliation rate — operational baseline [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    • Labour courts have the highest conciliation rate in the Brazilian judiciary.
    • First instance (varas do trabalho): 42.9% of cases resolved by agreement in 2019 — >853,000 settlements totaling >R$14.4 billion (TST Relatório da Conciliação 2019).
    • Overall rate across Justiça do Trabalho: ~24% (CNJ Justiça em Números 2019).
    • Court units: 1,585 varas do trabalho; 610 municipalities with labor-court presence (CNJ 2024).
    • Vacancy rate: 11.6% (lowest of all branches).
    • Congestion rate: 44.1% (lowest of all branches).

    Implication for connect: settlement is the modal disposition alongside partial-win. Outcome coding must distinguish acordo from julgamento — a judge favouring a connected lawyer can either rule favourably or steer the parties to a favourable settlement.

    Direitos indisponíveis [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    • Grounded in CF.7. The 2017 reform clarified the boundary:
      • CLT.611-A: negotiable via collective bargaining (work schedules, banco de horas, telework, etc.).
      • CLT.611-B: non-negotiable (salário mínimo, FGTS + 40% multa, 13º, férias + 1/3, adicionais, aviso prévio, saúde e segurança, etc.).
    • In-court settlements may discount disputed amounts but cannot waive proven indisposable rights — judges verify during homologation.
    • Context only for connect: shapes what kinds of settlement compositions are feasible, but not load-bearing for identification.

    Typical first-instance timeline [NEW — from shared reference, needs review]

    Based on aggregated CNJ/TRT data:

    • Filing → first hearing: ~135 days (~4.5 months).
    • First hearing → continuation hearing: ~300–450 days (high variance).
    • Continuation → sentença: ~60 days.
    • Fase de conhecimento total: ~1–2 years.
    • Fase de execução: ~4 years 11 months (CNJ 2015 data).
    • Overall average duration: ~2 years 4 months.

    Implication for connect: the project's reported median filing-to-decision of 214 days is well below the aggregate average — likely driven by settlement selection (cases that settle early resolve fast). 97% decided within the data coverage period is high relative to the aggregate.
