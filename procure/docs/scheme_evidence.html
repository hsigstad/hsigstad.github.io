Scheme Evidence

Scheme Evidence from Case Studies

Anecdotal and case-level evidence on corruption scheme mechanics, drawn from TJSP sentenças, CADE decisions, Lava Jato denúncias, and institutional guides. Each entry cites the specific source document.

1. Fracionamento (Contract Splitting)

Legal framework: Prohibited by Art. 23 §5 of Lei 8.666/1993 and Art. 75 §1 of Lei 14.133/2021. Exploits the dispensa thresholds (Art. 24, I: R$15,000 for works; Art. 24, II: R$8,000 for purchases) and the convite modality thresholds (Art. 23, I, b).

Case examples

General Salgado (TJSP 0001731-89.2009.8.26.0204): Municipality split urban infrastructure work into two contracts (R$12,920 + R$5,225) to stay below the R$15,000 dispensa threshold (Art. 24, I). The second contract explicitly stated it was a "complement" to the first. Prefeito Iaucir Carlos Marques involved.

Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Construction contracts split to avoid tomada de preços (Art. 23, I, b), using carta-convite (Art. 22 §3) instead. Contracts initiated without basic engineering projects. Revealed by TCE reports and testimony from "invited" firms who never actually competed. Prefeito Lairton Gomes Goulart, Secretary Lucília Goulart, Director Tupi Rodrigues involved.

Divinolândia/São Sebastião da Grama (TJSP 1000487-42.2017.8.26.0588): Fragmented IT equipment purchases via successive verbal contracts below dispensa threshold (Art. 24, II). Products belonged to a single "group of necessity." Revealed by criminal confessions from the business owner and absence of formal records. Prefeito Ismar Ernani de Oliveira involved.

Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Construction materials for popular housing purchased through successive cartas-convites in small batches, avoiding tomada de preços. Related/shell companies won all lots. Grafotechnic examinations revealed forged signatures on invitation documents. Prefeito José Aparecido de Oliveira involved.

Jales (TJSP 1005280-19.2020.8.26.0297): Vehicle fleet insurance fragmented into multiple small contracts to enable direct contracting with a specific broker. Prices 40% above market rate. Revealed by internal audit and TCE reports. Head of Procurement Nelson Guzzo Júnior involved.

Detection indicators

Per TCU Referencial de Combate a Fraude (Anexo 13) and CADE Guia (Section II.4):

  • Temporal proximity: successive contracts with same object and vendor
  • Threshold bunching: contract values just below dispensa/convite limits
  • Absence of consolidated annual procurement plans
  • Vendor concentration: single firm or related firms winning series of small contracts

2. Empresas de Fachada / Empresas Fantasma (Shell/Phantom Companies)

Legal framework: Use of fraudulent entities prohibited by Lei 8.666/1993 Art. 90 (criminalizes frustrating competitive nature of bidding); Lei 12.846/2013 Art. 5, IV, e (prohibits creating entity in fraudulent manner to participate in bidding); Lei 14.133/2021 Art. 160 (allows disregard of legal personality to expose illicit acts).

Roles in procurement fraud

Per CADE Guia (Section II.3.1) and TCU Referencial (Anexo 13), shell companies serve three primary roles:

  1. Cover bidding: submit pro forma bids with errors or intentionally high prices to make the favored bid look legitimate.
  2. Market division recompense: firms that "withdraw" from auction are later subcontracted by the winner to distribute cartel profits.
  3. Simulation of modality requirements: in carta-convite (Art. 22 §3 of Lei 8.666/1993, minimum 3 invited firms), officials use shell companies to fill the quota.

Case examples

São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 1003519-62.2015.8.26.0576): Entrepreneur José Luis Andreossi created Mário Morales Navarro Construtora, registered in the name of a rural worker ("laranja") with no business experience. Used to simulate competition against Andreossi's legitimate firm in municipalities including Uchoa, Cedral, and Ipiguá. Revealed by confessions from the laranja and engineering representative; seized digital media showed Andreossi managed finances of both firms simultaneously.

Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Companies FT Construções e Comércio Tarabai and HMI Engenharia were part of a group controlled by "Chiquinho do CDHU." Submitted cover bids in construction projects for popular housing; some firms participated without proper accreditation. Revealed by employee testimony on pre-arranged winners and grafotechnic examinations proving forged signatures on bidding documents.

Pinhalzinho (TJSP 0000703-26.2015.8.26.0447): Centro Diagnósticos Atibaia and Clínica Médica Negrini linked through family members and shared management. Simulated competition during dispensations of bidding to direct contracts to pre-determined beneficiary. Revealed by identical addresses and partners with direct family ties.

Lava Jato SP — Rodoanel/DERSA (denúncia CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO, MPF): A "financial nucleus" used laranjas and shell entities as interposed persons (interpostas pessoas jurídicas). Beyond bid-rigging, entities were used to launder bribe payments to DERSA officials. Revealed by Odebrecht's "drousys" system recording structured illicit payments; bank records showed accounts used solely as transit points with no real commercial purpose.

Operação Munditia (GAECO, Apr 2024): PCC-linked criminal network controlled front companies providing outsourced labor (cleaning, security, maintenance) to 13+ municipal governments (Guarulhos, Cubatão, Sorocaba, and others). Companies from the same economic group posed as independent competitors. Contracts exceeding R$200M. Money laundering through cash, checks (R$3.5M seized), and dollars. Represents organized-crime-driven variant where a criminal faction seeks legitimate revenue streams through shell companies in procurement.

Operação Dolos (GAECO, 2019+): 24 firms specialized in school uniforms and materials formed a cartel across 25 municipalities and 12 prefeituras. Shell companies (empresas de fachada) at same physical address; nominees (laranjas) as formal owners to hide real controllers. Scale: R$40M+.

Detection indicators

Per MPSP Manual (p. 36) and CADE Guia (pp. 47-48):

  • Identical bid documents: proposals from "competing" firms with same spelling errors, identical formatting, or similar handwriting
  • Information frictions: bidders having non-public knowledge of each other's pricing or sharing phone numbers
  • Creation dates: firms created shortly before an edital is published, or lacking history in the relevant industry
  • Laranja profiles: partners who are rural workers, domestic employees, or individuals with no assets compatible with the contract value

3. Cartel Coordination in Large Procurement

Evidence on how cartels organized bidding coordination, drawn from CADE decisions and Lava Jato SP denúncias.

Communication mechanisms

Merenda Escolar (CADE PA 08012.007149/2009-39, merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Members held formal meetings in Nutriplus offices and hotels. Created a trade union ("Sindimerenda") as a lawful front to justify frequent contact.

Trens e Metrôs (CADE PA 08700.004617/2013-41, trens_metros_voto.txt): Coordination facilitated by specialized consultants (Procint and Constech) who acted as intermediaries, organizing meetings and proposing market division models. Meetings in Alstom's workshops and hotels.

Petrobras Edificações (CADE PA 08700.002086/2015-14, petrobras_edificacoes_historico_conduta.pdf): In-person meetings, often at Queiroz Galvão headquarters, supplemented by phone calls and SMS.

Rodoanel/Sisviário (MPF, FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): The "G5" (Andrade Gutierrez, Camargo Corrêa, OAS, Odebrecht, Queiroz Galvão) held meetings in their own headquarters or construction sites.

Winner assignment rules

Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Rotation (rodízio) and geographic market division — companies stayed in specific regions of SP state.

Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Assignment based on original equipment manufacturers (privileging the firm that built the train for its maintenance) or "political" agreements to accommodate all "large" and "medium" players.

Petrobras Edificações (petrobras_edificacoes_historico_conduta.pdf): Contracts allocated according to joint commercial interests of the club, expanding the group to 11–12 firms as needed to neutralize new competitors.

Rodoanel/Sisviário (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): The G5 used a lottery (sorteio) to choose lots shortly before submitting proposals.

Cover bidding techniques

Per CADE Guia Section II.3.1 (p. 33) and OECD Guidelines (p. 2):

Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Losing firms engaged in bid suppression (withdrawing during oral bidding phase) or submitted pro-forma bids with similar prices to block outsiders.

Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Firms submitted deliberately high prices (sometimes 50% above market) or purposefully included disqualifying errors, such as missing CREA registration documents.

Petrobras Edificações (petrobras_edificacoes_historico_conduta.pdf): Companies provided propostas de cobertura or suppressed their bids to ensure the pre-selected consortium won.

Rodoanel/Sisviário (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): Firms used "Amor vs. Briga" (Love vs. Fight) spreadsheets, where "Amor" prices were inflated by R$50–100 million per lot compared to competitive "Briga" prices.

Compensation of non-winners

Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0867681.txt): Cash payments (por fora) recorded in parallel spreadsheets, sometimes disguised as internal expense sharing.

Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Primary mechanism was subcontracting the losers or forming "sham" consortia where the winner shared a slice of the project faturamento. Described in CADE Guia Section II.3.6 (pp. 45–46).

Petrobras Edificações (petrobras_edificacoes_historico_conduta.pdf): One firm (WTorre) was compensated with financial payments to stop lowering its price during direct negotiations with Petrobras.

Rodoanel/Sisviário (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): Firms not part of winning consortia were rewarded with subcontracting rights for approximately 25% of work value.

Coercion and intimidation

Operação Prenunciado I–III (GAECO, 2022–2023): Bariri municipal procurement racket used physical intimidation: military police officers stationed at bidding sessions to threaten and coerce potential competitors from participating. Violence and serious threats against potential whistleblowers. Sentences of 10–22 years. Represents an extreme variant where physical coercion replaces economic incentives for maintaining cartel discipline — likely leaves distinct traces in procurement data (very few bidders, no competitive pressure at all).

Enforcement within cartels

Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Compliance monitored through building reception logs (tracking meeting attendance) and seized spreadsheets tracking payments and regional dominance.

Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Firms used threats of "chumbo cruzado" (cross-fire) — if one firm cheated, others would launch technical and legal appeals to disqualify the cheater's other projects.

Rodoanel/Sisviário (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): Officials like Paulo Vieira de Souza enforced the cartel by warning that firms showing "lack of goodwill" in renegotiating current contracts would be excluded from future project packages.

Role of public officials

Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Officials shaped edital specifications (technical criteria) to favor cartel members and convinced municipalities to switch to outsourced models.

Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Officials leaked confidential studies and edital details before publication; allowed "re-consortia" — permitting firms to join forces after they had already been pre-qualified as individual competitors.

Rodoanel/Sisviário (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): DERSA officials provided privileged technical drawings and "administered" the market, even changing rules mid-stream to allow creation of SPEs to accommodate non-winning firms.

Legal provisions

  • Bid rigging: Art. 36 §3, I, d of Lei 12.529/2011; Art. 90 of Lei 8.666/1993 (now Art. 337-F of the Penal Code via Lei 14.133/2021)
  • Subcontracting abuse: CADE Guia Section II.3.6 (pp. 45–46); OECD Guidelines p. 1
  • Cover bidding: CADE Guia Section II.3.1 (p. 33); OECD Guidelines p. 2

4. Kickback / Propina Payment Systems

Evidence on how bribe payments were structured, channeled, and concealed, drawn from Lava Jato SP denúncias, CADE decisions, and TJSP sentenças.

Calculation of amounts

Fixed percentages:

  • Metrô de São Paulo (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Bribes calculated as 0.5% of monthly measurements (medições) paid to contractors. Other records suggest 0.9% of total contract value.

Variable/market-based:

  • Rodoanel Sul (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): Bribe cost embedded in "Amor vs. Briga" pricing — "Amor" prices R$50–100 million higher per lot than competitive "Briga" prices.

Lump sums:

  • Paraibuna (TJSP 1000331-50.2015.8.26.0418): Bribe was a specific lump sum of R$9,000.

Payment channels

Cash in person:

  • Metrô-SP (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Envelopes containing R$50,000– 60,000 delivered at restaurants ("Amadeus," "A Bela Sintra," "Café Antique").

Asset transfers:

  • DERSA (denuncia-dersa.pdf): Bribes paid as physical housing units (CDHU apartments) transferred to "ghost" residents who were employees of the official's family.

Luxury goods:

  • São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 3009633-51.2013.8.26.0576): Bribes included luxury vehicles (VW Passat, Land Rover) and international trips to China.

Timing relative to contract

During execution:

  • Metrô-SP: Payments made monthly as percentage of work actually measured and paid by the state.

Pre-homologation:

  • Paraibuna (TJSP 1000331-50.2015.8.26.0418): Payment deposited into Mayor's account before official homologation of the bidding process.

Prior to award (commitment):

  • Metrô-SP: "Pre-commitments" made as condition for officials to maintain and adit contracts from 1990 without launching new mandatory bidding processes.

Financial intermediaries

Company executives:

  • Metrô-SP/Rodoanel: Directors like Fabio Gandolfo (Odebrecht) personally carried cash to officials (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf).

Professional money changers (doleiros):

  • As schemes matured (~2006), companies like Odebrecht moved toward using professional doleiros, eliminating need for direct executive involvement in deliveries.

Family members:

  • DERSA (denuncia-dersa.pdf): Official's daughter (Tatiana) and a subordinate's sister served as intermediaries to register laranjas for housing units and cash out payments.

Concealment mechanisms

Simulated reimbursements:

  • Paraibuna: Bribe masked as reimbursement for out-of-pocket expenses allegedly incurred by the Mayor on behalf of the city.

Laundering via luxury goods:

  • São José do Rio Preto: Official received car as gift, sold it, used proceeds to buy a different vehicle in his own name to break paper trail.

Subcontracting invoices:

  • Per OECD Guidelines (p. 1) and CADE Guia Section II.3.6: Primary concealment method is fraudulent invoices for non-existent subcontracting or consulting work.

Detection evidence

Structured information systems:

  • Odebrecht "Drousys" and "MyWebDay" systems recorded programmed payments to codenames like "Brasileiro" (Sérgio Brasil) (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf, denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf).

Plea/collaboration agreements:

  • Testimony from collaborators like Sérgio Correa Brasil and Benedicto Junior provided context for financial records.

Internal audits and bank records:

  • DERSA audits identified housing units assigned to people with zero geographic link to construction areas; bank records showed immediate cash-outs of "indemnities" by relatives of officials (denuncia-dersa.pdf).

Legal provisions

  • Peculato: Art. 312 of the Penal Code
  • Insertion of false data: Art. 313-A of the Penal Code
  • Improbidade administrativa: Arts. 9, 10, and 11 of Lei 8.429/1992

5. Superfaturamento (Overpricing and Cost Manipulation)

Evidence on how contract prices were inflated and overbilling implemented, drawn from TJSP sentenças, CADE decisions, Lava Jato denúncias, and institutional guides (CGU and TCU).

Typology (per CGU Guia Prático)

Per CGU Guia Prático (Chapter 2.A and Chapter 3):

Sobrepreço: Occurs at contracting stage — price budgeted for a bid or contracted price expressly superior to market reference prices.

Superfaturamento na execução: Actual damage to public treasury that materializes during contract performance.

Jogo de planilha: Per CGU Guia Section 3.C and Art. 6, LVII of Lei 14.133/2021 — alterations in engineering works budget causing economic-financial imbalance in favor of the contractor. Involves increasing quantities of high-margin items while decreasing low-margin items through amendments.

Price inflation mechanisms

Inflated reference prices:

  • Vargem Grande do Sul (TJSP 1000316-79.2020.8.26.0653): Food prices set 19.5% above market value.
  • Jales (TJSP 1005280-19.2020.8.26.0297): Insurance premiums 40% higher than market rates due to direct contracting with intermediary broker.

Rigged cost estimates:

  • Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Internal Siemens documents admitted that Line 5 prices were "very high" because the "political" consortium environment allowed prices impossible in genuine competition.

BDI manipulation:

  • Trens e Metrôs: Cartel members coordinated pre-filling of price spreadsheets, including specific BDI (Bonificação e Despesas Indiretas) percentages, to ensure all bids uniformly high.

Phantom quantities/items:

  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Companies received payments for more materials or services than actually utilized.

Abuse of contract amendments (aditivos)

Per Art. 65 of Lei 8.666/1993 (limits: 25% for purchases/services, 50% for construction renovations):

Rodoanel Norte / Operação Pedra (ftlj_sp_denuncia_operacao_pedra.pdf): Officials and contractors signed aditivos to include "new" services for rock removal (matacões). Investigation revealed these conditions were already predicted in the basic project — aditivos were unnecessary and solely intended to inflate contract value.

Pinhalzinho (TJSP 0000703-26.2015.8.26.0447): Emergency contract illegally extended twice beyond maximum period allowed by law to avoid new competitive tender.

Metrô de São Paulo (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): To avoid mandatory new bidding, officials signed over 20 terms of extension between 1990 and 2004 for contracts that had not yet begun execution.

Measurement fraud (medições)

Simulated repairs:

  • São Pedro (TJSP 0003911-34.2011.8.26.0584): Fraudulent invoices for vehicle repairs that never took place.

Incomplete delivery:

  • Itaí (TJSP 1006003-14.2018.8.26.0263): Contractor received full payment in advance for drilling a well that was never finished.

Kickback anchoring:

  • Metrô-SP (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Bribes calculated as 0.5% of monthly medições paid to contractors, incentivizing officials to approve measurements regardless of actual progress.

Overpricing magnitudes

Case Margin Source
Merenda Escolar CADE adopted 20% for fines; FIPE estimated 72% vs self-managed meals merenda_voto_0862253.txt
Trens e Metrôs 16–25% higher in cartel rounds vs. round 3 (genuine competition) trens_metros_voto.txt
Rodoanel Sul R$50–100M per lot above competitive prices ("Amor vs. Briga") FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf
Vargem Grande do Sul 19.5% above market TJSP 1000316-79.2020.8.26.0653
Jales 40% above market (insurance) TJSP 1005280-19.2020.8.26.0297

Detection methods

Price benchmarking:

  • MPSP Manual (p. 36) and CGU Guia (Chapter 4.1.vii): "Basket of Acceptable Prices" method comparing to Compras.gov.br or Banco de Preços em Saúde.

Statistical screening:

  • TCU (TC 005.920/2022-1): Identified "Asphalt Cartel" by noticing average winning discounts plummeted from 24.5% to 5% when specific firms dominated.

TCE inspections and engineering audits:

  • Bertioga: TCE-SP reports flagged lack of basic projects and fragmented contracts as primary indicator of fraud.

Forensic information systems:

  • Odebrecht "Drousys" system recorded illicit overpricing payments programmed into contract measurements (Lava Jato SP denúncias).

6. Direcionamento de Licitação (Bid Steering / Specification Rigging)

Manipulation of the procurement process to ensure a specific winner, violating the principle of competition required by Art. 3 §1, I of Lei 8.666/1993 (and Art. 9, I, a of Lei 14.133/2021).

Edital manipulation

Restrictive clauses and brand specifications:

  • Vargem Grande do Sul (TJSP 1000316-79.2020.8.26.0653): Edital for school meals included restrictive clauses and brand-specific requirements limiting competition to a group of companies with shared partners.

Corruption of officials to insert directed clauses:

  • Operação Dolos (GAECO, 2019+): Firms corrupted municipal officials to insert directed clauses in editais for school uniforms/materials; price-fixing among firms to divide lots across 25 municipalities.

Influenced edital formatting:

  • Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Cartel members influenced municipalities to adopt outsourced meal models and influenced edital formatting to include technical criteria (e.g., specific "bromatological reports") as entry barriers for non-cartel firms.

Firms drafting their own specifications:

  • Rodoanel Sul (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): DERSA officials allowed the "G5" group to conduct the technical studies used to draft the edital, letting firms include engineering difficulties and methods that favored their expertise and excluded others.

Information asymmetry

Advance knowledge of editais:

  • CPTM maintenance rounds (trens_metros_voto.txt): Companies were discussing market division and price scenarios based on train series (S7000, S2100, etc.) in February 2011, more than a year before the edital was released in 2012.

Prior exchange of bid details:

  • Metrô de São Paulo (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Prior exchange of details regarding upcoming bids between Metrô-SP agents and executives from Odebrecht and other firms.

Comissão de licitação complicity

Undue disqualification of competitors:

  • Pinhalzinho (TJSP 0000703-26.2015.8.26.0447): During "Pregão 11/2013," commission unduly disqualified Centro Diagnósticos over manipulated technicalities to ensure victory of pre-determined winner Medgroup.

Accepting irregular documentation from favored firms:

  • Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Commission accepted bids from HMI Engenharia despite lacking proper accreditation and submitting flawed documentation, solely to populate the list of "competitors."

Skipping mandatory procedures:

  • Itaí (TJSP 1006003-14.2018.8.26.0263): President of Bidding Commission and Legal Advisor adjusted process to ensure CIMAPER won the artesian well contract, skipping mandatory price research.

Convite manipulation

Per Art. 22 §3 of Lei 8.666/1993 (minimum 3 invited firms):

Forged invitations:

  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Administration used carta-convite to direct contracts to favored firms. In some cases, two of three required "competitors" never actually received invitations; their signatures on bidding documents were forged.

Successive invitations to avoid transparency:

  • Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Municipality used successive invitation letters for materials to avoid the more transparent tomada de preços, ensuring the same family-linked group won all lots.

Interaction with other schemes

With fracionamento:

  • Bertioga: Contract splitting was the tool used to lower individual values so the administration could legally use the convite modality, which is more easily directed.

With cartel:

  • Rodoanel Sul: Cartel utilized directional specifications to "administer the market," ensuring specific lots fell to specific pre-selected winners.

Detection indicators

Per TCU Referencial (Anexo 13) and CADE Guia (pp. 26–27, 47–49):

  • Targeted specs: specifications matching exact catalog description of winner's product
  • Unfair disqualifications: disqualifying bidders for "merely formal" errors (violating Art. 12, III of Lei 14.133/2021)
  • Post-award changes: significant changes to contract specifications shortly after favored firm is hired
  • Unusual barriers: edital requirements for high technical experience or capital social disproportionate to actual work (violating Art. 37, XXI of CF/88)
  • Administrative omission: failure to follow technical opinions from internal legal or engineering departments that flag irregularities

7. Durable Networks and Repeat Relationships

Evidence that corruption in procurement is organized through long-term, stable networks between clusters of firms and officials, persisting across decades and political cycles.

Longevity

Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): CADE identified a national rail cartel operating for 15 years (1998–2013), spanning four states and at least 26 major bidding processes.

Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): School meal procurement cartel in São Paulo state operated for at least 20 years.

Metrô-SP (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Network maintained contracts originally signed in 1990 through 20+ terms of extension (aditivos), with illicit payments continuing through at least 2007.

Rodoanel Sul (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): The "G5" group maintained coordinated market control from 2004 to at least 2015.

Repeat firm-official pairs

DERSA and the G5 (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): DERSA directors (Paulo Vieira de Souza, Mário Rodrigues Júnior) maintained multi-year relationships with the G5 (Andrade Gutierrez, Camargo Corrêa, OAS, Odebrecht, Queiroz Galvão).

Metrô-SP and Odebrecht (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Manager Sérgio Correa Brasil received bribes (0.5% of monthly measurements) from Odebrecht executives Fábio Gandolfo and Benedicto Júnior over several years for the same project.

Jales (TJSP 1005280-19.2020.8.26.0297): Head of Procurement Nelson Guzzo Júnior repeatedly directed insurance contracts to Ademar Bocalon Rodrigues and his firm (TUTA) without competition.

Avaré (TJSP 1002632-98.2016.8.26.0073): Nutricionale Comércio de Alimentos repeatedly contracted for school meals via emergency dispensations authorized by Prefeito Rogélio Barchetti Urrêa.

Caconde (TJSP 0001827-77.2013.8.26.0103): Municipal vehicles repeatedly washed at businesses owned by Reginaldo Araújo Pinto across two different administrations (Antônio Carlos de Faria and Luciano Semensato) — network surviving administration change.

Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Prefeito Lairton Goulart and Director Tupi Rodrigues repeatedly favored firms "Rubão Ltda" and "Construpac" through artificial fracionamento.

Cross-municipality networks

Merenda cartel (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Network of food companies (Nutriplus, SP Alimentação, Convida) implemented the same rotation and price-fixing scheme across 30 different municipalities in São Paulo state.

Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): National cartel coordinating bids across São Paulo, Distrito Federal, Minas Gerais, and Rio Grande do Sul.

José Luis Andreossi (TJSP 1003519-62.2015.8.26.0576): Entrepreneur created phantom company to simulate competition against his legitimate firm across municipalities of Uchoa, Cedral, and Ipiguá.

PCC-linked firms (Operação Munditia, GAECO Apr 2024): Criminal network controlled front companies providing outsourced labor across 13+ municipalities (Guarulhos, Cubatão, Sorocaba, Ferraz de Vasconcelos, Arujá, Santa Isabel, Poá, Jaguariúna, Guarujá, Buri, Itatiba, and others). Systematic corruption of officials (secretaries, council presidents, purchasing agents). Contracts exceeding R$200M.

Family and personal ties

Pinhalzinho (TJSP 0000703-26.2015.8.26.0447): Fraudulent competition simulated between Centro Diagnósticos Atibaia and Clínica Médica Negrini — same address, partners with direct family ties.

Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Network centered around "Chiquinho do CDHU" using firm owned by his wife (FT Construções) to win directed lots.

Macaubal (TJSP 0000098-31.2015.8.26.0334): Prefeita Marli Teixeira directed contracts to businesses owned by her niece and other relatives.

Hortolândia (TJSP 0004993-69.2009.8.26.0229): Three "competitors" (Viação Princesa D'Oeste, Álamo Turismo, Salamanca) shared the same owners and simulated competition.

DERSA (denuncia-dersa.pdf): Director Paulo Vieira de Souza used his daughter (Tatiana) and subordinate's sister as financial intermediaries and laranjas to register assets obtained from diverted funds.

Network size

Large-scale (10+ actors):

  • Trens e Metrôs (CADE): 19 firms and 67 people.
  • Rodoanel Sul (Lava Jato): 33 accused individuals including 18 firms.
  • Ferraz de Vasconcelos (TJSP 0003499-21.2011.8.26.0191): Cerqueira Torres coordinated with 10 other firms in a local construction cartel.

Bilateral arrangements:

  • General Salgado (TJSP 0001731-89.2009.8.26.0204): Single prefeito and one contractor.

Network resilience

Surviving administration changes (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO .pdf): When new state government took office in 2007 and ordered contract re-evaluations (Decreto Estadual 51.473), the network adapted by renegotiating existing Rodoanel contracts in exchange for pre-selected lots in a new "Sistema Viário" package.

Iterative "rounds" (trens_metros_voto.txt): Train cartel "renewed" coordination for each new maintenance cycle. The 2nd Round (2006–2008) saw firms meet to update the 1st Round's agreement to accommodate newer firms like MGE and MPE.

Bypassing new bids (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Officials signed over 20 aditivos to keep 1990 contracts alive for decades, avoiding requirement for new tenders.

Organizational hierarchy

Hub-and-spoke with intermediaries (trens_metros_voto.txt): Train cartel organized around consultancy Procint as hub for meetings and market division models. Alstom identified as "Leader" exercising centralizing role for sensitive information.

Public official as market administrator (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): Paulo Vieira de Souza (DERSA) acted as hub, stating: "The market is a problem. I manage it. I take care of the market" — dictating which lot each G5 firm would win.

Horizontal coordination via unions (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Merenda escolar cartel used creation of trade union ("Sindimerenda") as front for horizontal peer-to-peer coordination meetings.

Hierarchical chain of command (denuncia-dersa.pdf): Clear command chain in DERSA resettlement scheme: Paulo Vieira de Souza (Director) → Geraldo Vilela (Dept. Head) → ex-funcionária (contractor staff) who inserted false data into systems.

Legal provisions

  • Frustrating competition: Art. 90 of Lei 8.666/1993 (now Art. 337-F of the Penal Code)
  • Bid rigging: Art. 36 §3, I, d of Lei 12.529/2011
  • Abuse of aditivos: Art. 65 of Lei 8.666/1993
  • TCU Referencial (Anexo 13): "evidence of close relationship between bidders" and "patterns in winning/losing bids" as primary red flags

8. Legal Thresholds and Strategic Exploitation

Evidence on how procurement modality thresholds and dispensation rules were exploited by corrupt actors.

Threshold values

Lei 8.666/1993 (Art. 23):

  • Engineering (Art. 23, I): Convite up to R$150,000; Tomada de Preços up to R$1,500,000; Concorrência above R$1,500,000.
  • Goods/services (Art. 23, II): Convite up to R$80,000; Tomada de Preços up to R$650,000; Concorrência above R$650,000.
  • Updated via Decreto 9.412/2018.

Dispensations under Lei 8.666/1993 (Art. 24, I–II):

  • Art. 24, I (works/engineering): up to 10% of convite limit (originally R$15,000).
  • Art. 24, II (other): up to 10% of convite limit (originally R$8,000).

Lei 14.133/2021 (Art. 75):

  • Art. 75, I (works/engineering/vehicle maintenance): up to R$100,000.
  • Art. 75, II (goods/other services): up to R$50,000.
  • Updated annually via Decree. Significantly higher than Lei 8.666 thresholds.

Modality shopping

Convite instead of tomada de preços:

  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Administration fractioned works to avoid tomada de preços and adopt carta-convite, directing contracts to specific firms while forging required three competitors' signatures.

Dispensa instead of convite:

  • General Salgado (TJSP 0001731-89.2009.8.26.0204): Mayor split single infrastructure project into two contracts (R$12,920 + R$5,225) to stay below R$15,000 dispensa limit (Art. 24, I).
  • Divinolândia (TJSP 1000487-42.2017.8.26.0588): IT equipment purchases belonging to single group of necessity split into multiple small verbal contracts to remain within dispensa threshold.

Threshold bunching

Per TCU Referencial (Anexo 13): "contratação com dispensa com valor logo abaixo do limite" (contracting via dispensation with value just below the limit) is a specific red flag for fraud.

Codevasf case (TCU TC 005.920): Engefort firm won numerous lots with discounts of only 0.01% to 1%, keeping contract value at absolute maximum allowed for each bidding process.

Emergency dispensation abuse (Art. 24, IV)

Fabricated emergencies:

  • São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 3009633-51.2013.8.26.0576): Officials fabricated emergency grounds for waste collection services to favor Constroeste after intentionally breaking a previous contract.
  • Paraibuna (TJSP 1000331-50.2015.8.26.0418): Formal failures in tractor procurement justified as "emergency" due to heavy rains, masking directed contract where the Mayor received R$9,000 bribe.
  • São Paulo capital (GAECO/TCM-SP investigation, 2024–2025): 303 emergency contracts totaling R$5 billion under the Nunes administration. "Fabricated emergencies" where urgency was artificial. Suspected cartel among contractors receiving no-bid emergency contracts. TCM-SP audit flagged R$67M in suspected superfaturamento across 36 contracts for "emergency" works done without licitação.

Deserto manipulation (Art. 24, V)

Corrupt officials or cartels ensure "deserted" auctions by including restrictive or impossible clauses in the edital, discouraging legitimate firms from participating. Once the auction fails, the administration hires a pre-selected firm directly under Art. 24, V.

Inexigibilidade abuse (Art. 25)

Fraudulent sole-source claims:

  • Jales (TJSP 1005280-19.2020.8.26.0297): Municipality bypassed competitive bidding for vehicle insurance using fracionamento to facilitate direct non-competitive hiring of a broker, prices 40% above market.
  • Per MPSP Manual (p. 86): Inexigibilidade often used to hire "notorious specialists" or "exclusive representatives" who are actually part of the corruption network.

9. Improbidade Case Selection and Judicial Outcomes

Evidence on how improbidade cases are triggered, prosecuted, and decided, drawn from TJSP sentenças and institutional sources.

Investigation triggers

Audit court reports (TCE-SP):

  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075) and Itaí (TJSP 1006003-14.2018.8.26.0263): TCE-SP findings of irregularity in accounts or specific contracts triggered MP civil inquiries.

Media and citizen reports:

  • São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 1003512-70.2015.8.26.0576): Press reports about suspicious companies led to investigation.

Police investigations:

  • Jales: Joint Federal Police operations revealed schemes through criminal search and seizure.

Proactive monitoring:

  • Per MPSP Manual: MP should proactively monitor transparency portals and systems like Licitacon, not rely solely on external triggers.

Case timeline

Investigation phase:

  • Notícia de Fato (NF): must be processed within 120 days.
  • Inquérito Civil (IC): should conclude in 1 year, frequently extended.

Judicial phase:

  • Typical duration from filing to first-instance sentença: 7–8 years.
  • Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): 8 years.
  • São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 1003519-62.2015.8.26.0576): 8 years.
  • CNJ study: average time to judgment in state courts is 1,586 days. Source: CNJ study cited in MPSP Manual.

Improbidade categories in procurement cases

Art. 9 (enriquecimento ilícito) — officials receiving direct benefits:

  • Paraibuna (TJSP 1000331-50.2015.8.26.0418): R$9,000 bribe deposited into Mayor's account.

Art. 10 (prejuízo ao erário) — most common in procurement:

  • Vargem Grande do Sul (TJSP 1000316-79.2020.8.26.0653): 19.5% overpricing causing financial loss to the municipality.

Art. 11 (violação de princípios) — residual category:

  • Acts violating impartiality or legality even if financial damage not proven, such as rigging technical specifications.

Conviction vs. acquittal factors

2021 reform impact (Lei 14.230/2021 Art. 1 §§1–3):

  • Eliminated culposa (negligent) improbidade; only intentional conduct (dolo) now actionable. Decisive factor in many outcomes.

Factors leading to acquittal:

  • Insufficient evidence of bad faith or proof that service was actually delivered (Itaí; Olímpia TJSP 1003924-73.2017.8.26.0400).

Factors leading to conviction:

  • Clear evidence of conluio (e.g., firms sharing addresses in Pinhalzinho).
  • Confessions from shell company owners.

Sanctions applied

Ressarcimento (restitution):

  • Mandatory in cases of damage, often calculated in later liquidation phase (Bertioga).

Suspension of political rights:

  • Typically 5 years for Mayors/officials in cases of directed contracts (São José do Rio Preto; Vargem Grande do Sul).

Fines and contracting prohibitions:

  • Civil fines (e.g., 20% of damage in Vargem Grande do Sul).
  • 3–5 year prohibitions on government contracting for involved firms.

Prescription issues

Current rule (Art. 23 of Lei 14.230/2021):

  • 8-year absolute prescription from occurrence of the fact.

Prescription vs. ressarcimento:

  • Bertioga: Most personal penalties (fines, suspension of rights) dismissed due to prescription, but defendants still ordered to pay ressarcimento.
  • STF (RE 852475): Ressarcimento for intentional improbidade is imprescritível (no statute of limitations).

10. Post-Award / Execution-Phase Corruption

Evidence on how corruption occurs during contract execution — after bidding is complete and the contract is awarded.

Contract amendments (aditivos) beyond superfaturamento

Avoiding new bidding:

  • Metrô de São Paulo (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Officials signed over 20 terms of extension to keep 1990 contracts alive through 2004, avoiding mandatory new bidding process after 14-year delay.

Unnecessary services as overpricing vehicle:

  • Rodoanel Norte / Operação Pedra (ftlj_sp_denuncia_operacao_pedra.pdf): Aditivos to include "new" services for rock removal (matacões) that were already predicted in the original basic project — amendments technically unnecessary, purely for overpricing.

Aditivos as bargaining chips:

  • Rodoanel Sul (FTLJSP_denuncia_CARTEL_RODOSUL_SISVIARIO.pdf): Officials used threat of withholding "goodwill" in future contracts to force current contract renegotiations, using aditivo process as bargaining chip for future bribes.

Legal basis: Art. 65 §1 of Lei 8.666/1993 (25% for services/purchases, 50% for renovations).

Subcontracting as profit distribution

Compensating cartel losers:

  • Per CADE Guia Section II.3.6 and TCU Referencial Anexo 13: Winning bidders subcontract "unsuccessful" bidders who submitted cover bids as compensation for their cartel role.
  • Metrô DF / Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Consortia agreed that winner would subcontract losing group for predetermined percentage (e.g., 48% vs. 52%) to maintain agreed market division.

Masking non-delivery:

  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Fragmented contracts allowed sub-contracted execution exceeding what was physically delivered, masking lack of basic engineering projects.

Fiscal and oversight failures

Complicit supervision:

  • Rodoanel Norte: Contract supervisors (fiscais) simply repeated contractor's arguments in official reports to justify fraudulent aditivos, failing to issue independent technical opinions.

Accepting inferior quality:

  • Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Bidding commission members and supervisors accepted materials and handiwork of inferior quality to maximize private group's profit at expense of public housing residents.

TCU red flags (Anexo 13): "Acceptance of invoices without proper oversight" and "non-application of sanctions despite low performance."

Non-delivery and phantom services

Fraudulent invoices:

  • São Pedro (TJSP 0003911-34.2011.8.26.0584): Fraudulent invoices for vehicle repairs never performed, using dispensa de licitação for paper trail.

Incomplete delivery with full payment:

  • Itaí (TJSP 1006003-14.2018.8.26.0263): Contractor received full payment for artesian well drilling never completed.

"Química" payments:

  • Per CGU Guia Section 3.G: "Contracted but unperformed services" used to justify payments for other services or acquisitions with no legal basis in the contract.

Payment manipulation

Advance payments without guarantees:

  • Itaí: Company received total payment in advance for unfinished work without mandatory guarantees. Per CGU Guia Section 3.D: advance payments without justification are superfaturamento because they deprive the state of time-value of money (taxa Selic).

Bribe anchoring to payments:

  • Metrô-SP (denuncia-metro-corrupcao.pdf): Kickbacks at 0.5% of monthly medições — bribes paid only as official approved contractor's billing.

Pre-homologation payments:

  • Paraibuna (TJSP 1000331-50.2015.8.26.0418): Checks issued and payments deposited into Mayor's account before bidding officially homologated.

Relationship to bidding-phase corruption

Continuation of bidding scheme:

  • Trens/metrôs and Rodoanel cases: Bidding-phase coordination (rotation, cover bidding) is the agreement on how to divide "spoils" extracted during execution via subcontracting and aditivos.

Independent opportunism:

  • Per CGU Guia Section 3: Execution fraud can occur independently through "jogo de planilha" (spreadsheet games) — firm wins legitimate bid then manipulates unit quantities in amendments to increase high-markup items and decrease low-markup items.

Legal provisions

  • Illegal modifications during execution: Art. 337-H of the Penal Code
  • Fraud in contract execution (inferior quality/quantity): Art. 337-L of the Penal Code

Empresas de fachada → construction: Shell companies simulate competitive requirements of complex modalities (tomada de preços), providing cover bids (Adamantina, São José do Rio Preto).

Superfaturamento → all sectors, different manifestation: In construction, through unnecessary aditivos for services already in project scope. In commodity purchases (food, fuel, medicine), through simple price inflation vs. market rates (Vargem Grande do Sul 19.5%, Jales 40%).

12. Actor Roles and Organizational Patterns

Evidence on the typical roles played by different actors in municipal procurement corruption, drawn from 50 TJSP sentenças and scheme evidence.

Who initiates

Public official initiated:

  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Administration initiated processes without basic projects to facilitate directed contracting.
  • São Pedro (TJSP 0003911-34.2011.8.26.0584): Chief of Staff "idealized" the scheme to forge vehicle repair invoices.

Private firm initiated:

  • São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 1003519-62.2015.8.26.0576): Entrepreneur José Luis Andreossi created shell companies to simulate competition across multiple municipalities.
  • São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 3009633-51.2013.8.26.0576): "Grupo Faria" approached a future official to request "favors" in exchange for gifts before he assumed office.

Municipal positions involved

Prefeito (Mayor): Provides overarching authorization or directs "emergency" justification. Cases: Pinhalzinho (TJSP 0000703-26.2015.8.26.0447), General Salgado (TJSP 0001731-89.2009.8.26.0204).

Secretário (Secretary): Initiates specific demand for goods/services. Cases: Bertioga (Secretária de Habitação), Várzea Paulista (Secretários de várias pastas).

Diretor (Director/Department Head): Manages technical flow of rigged bid. Cases: Avaré (Diretora de Alimentação Escolar), Caconde (Diretor de Turismo).

Assessor Jurídico (Legal Advisor): Provides "legal veneer" by issuing generic favorable opinions for dispensations or restrictive editais. Cases: Itaí (TJSP 1006003-14.2018.8.26.0263), Dracena (TJSP 0000555-52.2010.8.26 .0168).

Fiscal de Contrato (Contract Supervisor): Complicit in certifying work never performed or of inferior quality. Cases: Eldorado (Arquiteto Municipal), São José dos Campos (Fiscal Técnico).

Chefe de Gabinete (Chief of Staff): High-level intermediary. Cases: São Pedro (Aparecido Carrara), Chavantes (José Carlos Cadamuro).

Private sector roles

Active corruptors (proposing schemes, offering bribes):

  • "Grupo Faria" in São José do Rio Preto: paid for international trips and luxury cars for officials.
  • João Fragozo Júnior in Avaré: co-opted others to participate in fraudulent bid cluster.

Passive beneficiaries (winning directed contracts):

  • CIMAPER in Itaí: received directed contract for artesian well.
  • Nutricionale in Avaré: contracted repeatedly through "emergency" dispensations.

Cover bidders (shell companies submitting pro-forma bids):

  • Mario Morales Navarro Construtora in São José do Rio Preto: submitted higher bids to ensure intended winner chosen.
  • FT Construções in Adamantina: same role, filling minimum 3-bidder requirement for carta-convite (Art. 22 §3 of Lei 8.666/1993).

Intermediaries and facilitators

Brokers:

  • Jales (TJSP 1005280-19.2020.8.26.0297): Broker Ademar Bocalon Rodrigues served as central intermediary for insurance contracts, receiving illegal commissions.

Specialized consultants:

  • Procint in trens/metrôs cartel: organized meetings and proposed market division models among competitors (trens_metros_voto.txt).

Lawyers:

  • Olímpia (TJSP 1003924-73.2017.8.26.0400): Attorney hired as "administrative assessor" providing informal legal services that served Mayor's political promotion.

Typical network size

Bilateral arrangements (1 official + 1 firm):

  • General Salgado, Paraibuna.

Typical cluster (1–3 officials + 3–5 firms):

  • Common pattern to satisfy carta-convite modality requirements (minimum 3 invited firms).

Large-scale cartels (10+ actors):

  • Rodoanel Sul: 33 accused, 18 firms.
  • Ferraz de Vasconcelos (TJSP 0003499-21.2011.8.26.0191): Mayor, his brother, and cluster of 10 conluiadas firms.

  • Paulo Vieira de Souza (DERSA): daughter Tatiana and subordinate's sister as financial intermediaries.

Couriers:

  • Lower-level employees (company drivers) used to physically deliver cash bribes to restaurants or residences.

  • Corporate liability: Art. 5, IV of Lei 12.846/2013

13. Small vs. Large Municipality Patterns

Evidence on how corruption scheme complexity varies with municipality size, drawn from 50 TJSP sentenças.

Small municipalities (General Salgado, Macaubal, Caconde, etc.)

Predominant schemes: Fracionamento and direct hiring via fabricated dispensations. Goal is to stay below Art. 24, I–II thresholds (R$15,000 works, R$8,000 purchases).

Typical structure: Bilateral (1 official + 1 firm).

  • General Salgado (TJSP 0001731-89.2009.8.26.0204): Mayor split single project into two contracts to stay under R$15,000.
  • Caconde (TJSP 0001827-77.2013.8.26.0103): Vehicle washing directed to local politician's firm across multiple administrations.

Personal ties over shell companies: Family members used instead of sophisticated shells.

  • Macaubal (TJSP 0000098-31.2015.8.26.0334): Mayor directed carta-convite contracts to businesses owned by her niece.

Institutional weakness:

  • Divinolândia/São Sebastião da Grama (TJSP 1000487-42.2017.8.26.0588): Fragmented IT purchases via verbal contracts with total absence of formal records.
  • Itaí (TJSP 1006003-14.2018.8.26.0263): Assessor jurídico and bidding commission president collaborated to "adjust" tender without price research.
  • Per MPSP Manual (p. 25): Mayors returning for multiple non-consecutive mandates can lead to "normalization" of irregular practices.

Detection: Typically via TCE-SP reports on annual accounts or discovery of "impossible" services during standard audits.

Medium municipalities (Adamantina, Jales, Avaré, Tupã, Chavantes)

Increased complexity: Networks expand to 3–10 firms to satisfy minimum 3-bidder requirement of carta-convite (Art. 22 §3 of Lei 8.666/1993).

Regional intermediaries:

  • Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Regional broker "Chiquinho do CDHU" used wife's firm and several cover bidders to rig housing projects.
  • Jales (TJSP 1005280-19.2020.8.26.0297): Brokerage intermediary (TUTA) managed systematic fracionamento of insurance, prices 40% above market.

Administrative depth: More officials involved.

  • Avaré (TJSP 0000815-21.2013.8.26.0073): Mayor, garage directors, and multiple secretaries facilitating "favors" via fraudulent invoices.

Larger municipalities (São José do Rio Preto, Ferraz de Vasconcelos, Bertioga)

Shell company networks:

  • São José do Rio Preto (TJSP 1003519-62.2015.8.26.0576): Entrepreneur created network of shell companies using rural workers as laranjas to simulate competition across multiple municipalities (Uchoa, Cedral, Ipiguá).

Large-scale cartels:

  • Ferraz de Vasconcelos (TJSP 0003499-21.2011.8.26.0191): Construction cartel of 10 conluiadas firms coordinated to win successive carta-convite lots.

Financial sophistication: Cases involve COAF (financial intelligence) detecting illicit transfers between accounts of officials and firms.

Systemic aditivos:

  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Administration fragmented large works into dozens of small carta-convite lots to avoid tomada de preços (Art. 23, I, b), subsequently inflating values through unnecessary amendments.

Detection: Proactive intelligence operations (Operação Munditia — 13 cities), specialized engineering audits revealing jogo de planilha, plea agreements (Siemens, Odebrecht), or investigative journalism.

14. Evidence Types and Evidentiary Standards

Evidence on how corruption in procurement is proven in court, drawn from 50 TJSP sentenças, CADE decisions, and institutional guides. Per CADE Guia and OECD Guidelines: because cartels and corruption are clandestine, indirect evidence is often decisive.

Documentary evidence

Identical formatting and typos:

  • Taquaritinga (TJSP 1001856-70.2020.8.26.0619): Three bids had identical typos, revealing single author.
  • Miguelópolis (TJSP 1000922-11.2018.8.26.0352): Propostas used the same printer.

Parallel spreadsheets and internal records:

  • Merenda Escolar (merenda_voto_0862253.txt): Parallel spreadsheets found at SP Alimentação tracked "por fora" (off-book) payments and divided geographic dominance.

Forged signatures:

  • Adamantina (TJSP 0007153-26.2009.8.26.0081): Grafotechnic analysis of carta-convite invitation logs proved "competitors" never signed.
  • Bertioga (TJSP 1002040-48.2016.8.26.0075): Same pattern — forged invitation signatures.

Internal communications:

  • Trens e Metrôs (trens_metros_voto.txt): Emails discussing "political agreements" to avoid "price wars" proved anticompetitive intent.

Testimonial evidence

Confessions:

  • Divinolândia (TJSP 1000487-42.2017.8.26.0588): Business owner confessed to verbal contracts and fragmentation. Decisive.
  • Avaré (TJSP 0000815-21.2013.8.26.0073): Entrepreneur João Fragozo Júnior and partner detailed entire fraudulent invoice scheme.

Witness testimony:

  • Bertioga: Representatives of "invited" firms testified they never actually competed, proving process was simulated.

Leniency/collaboration agreements:

  • Siemens agreement provided roadmap for train cartel.
  • Sérgio Correa Brasil's testimony detailed bribes for Metrô-SP contract aditamentos.

Expert evidence (perícias)

Grafotechnic analysis:

  • Adamantina: Proved simulation of bidding documents through forged signatures.

Engineering audits:

  • Eldorado (TJSP 0001318-70.2012.8.26.0172): Perícia proved materials billed at rates significantly above market averages.

15. Quantitative Red Flags and Screening Indicators

Indicators recommended by institutional guides for screening procurement data to detect corruption. Sources: CADE Guia (Section II.4, pp. 47–49), TCU Referencial (Anexo 13), TCU Codevasf (TC 005.920), OECD Guidelines, MPSP Manual.

Bidding patterns

  • Low participation: Number of proposals substantially inferior to habitual or expected average for that object.
  • Bid spread: Suspicious margin between winning bid and others. In cover pricing, losers often add 10%+ to lowest bid.
  • Single-bidder incidence: High frequency of processes with only one bidder or only one qualified.
  • Unexpected deserto: Frequent "failed" auctions for specific objects, potentially manipulated to justify direct contracting (Art. 24, V of Lei 8.666/1993).
  • Sudden participation drops: Significant decrease in participants as certain firms begin to dominate.

Price indicators

  • Threshold clustering (bunching): Contract values just below legal thresholds (e.g., just under R$8,000 or R$15,000 under old law). Source: TCU Referencial Anexo 13.
  • Reference value proximity: Prices nearly identical to estimated/reference values, indicating lack of genuine downward competition.
  • Discount pattern shifts: TCU Codevasf (TC 005.920): Average winning discount plummeted from 24.5% to 5.32% over three years as "asphalt cartel" consolidated control.
  • Low-discount wins: Dominant firm repeatedly wins with negligible discounts (0.01% to 1%) against supposedly independent rivals. Source: TCU Codevasf.
  • Disparate regional prices: Drastically different prices for same object across different municipalities.

Winner concentration

  • Win rates: High win-to-participation ratios. TCU Codevasf: Engefort won 74.6% (47 of 63) of collusive lots.
  • Market share: Few suppliers winning same or similar quantities over multi-year series.
  • Geographic concentration: Firms winning only in certain geographic zones, indicating market division.

Participation patterns

  • Bid rotation (rodízio): Firms taking turns as winner across auctions or lots. Source: CADE Guia Section II.3.4.
  • Systematic withdrawals: Unexpected withdrawal without plausible explanation, often right before winning bid determined. Source: CADE Guia Section II.3.2.
  • Lot selection ("ballooning"): Firms bidding on all lots but submitting competitive prices for only one, providing cover on others.
  • Friendly pairing: Specific clusters of firms always bidding together across different municipalities.

Firm characteristics

  • Recent creation: Firms created shortly before edital publication or shortly before winning major contract. Source: CADE Guia pp. 47–48.
  • Shared infrastructure: Identical addresses, phone numbers, or email domains between "competing" firms. Source: MPSP Manual p. 36, CADE Guia pp. 47–48.
  • Laranja partners: Owners who are low-income individuals, domestic staff, or rural workers with no professional history in the industry.
  • Missing accreditation: Firms submitting bids despite lacking required technical accreditation, solely to populate bidder list.

Contract execution indicators

  • Excessive aditivos: Systematic amendments increasing contract values shortly after award, especially for services already predicted in basic project.
  • Advance payments: Payments before delivery/milestones without mandatory guarantees. Source: CGU Guia Section 3.D.
  • Subcontracting to losers: Winner subcontracts significant portion to "competitor" that lost or withdrew from same auction. Source: CADE Guia Section II.3.6 (pp. 45–46).
  • Phantom delivery: Payments for medições of work never performed or goods never received.
