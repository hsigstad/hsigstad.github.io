Hierarchical collusion

  • Tirole (1986)

    • topic: hierarchies and bureaucracies: on the role of collusion in organizations (J Law, Econ & Organization)
    • relevance: canonical three-tier principal-supervisor-agent model showing how coalition formation between supervisor and agent constrains optimal incentive design; foundational for understanding which organizational position enables which corruption form

  • Laffont and Martimort (1997)

    • topic: collusion under asymmetric information (Econometrica)
    • relevance: characterizes optimal collusion-proof mechanisms; preventing collusion requires distorting allocations, implying that anti-corruption safeguards in procurement have efficiency costs

  • Kofman and Lawarree (1993)

    • topic: collusion in hierarchical agency (Econometrica)
    • relevance: extends Tirole (1986) by endogenizing supervisor's collusion decision; shows that redundant monitors (e.g., external audits alongside internal supervisors) reduce collusion — mechanism for H10

  • Laffont and Tirole (1991)

    • topic: auction design and favoritism (Intl J Industrial Organization)
    • relevance: models how corrupt procurement official biases multi-attribute auctions toward favored supplier by manipulating quality weights

  • Laffont and Tirole (1993)

    • topic: A Theory of Incentives in Procurement and Regulation (MIT Press, book)
    • relevance: definitive treatment of mechanism design for procurement under adverse selection, moral hazard, and renegotiation; information asymmetry shapes both optimal and "optimally corrupt" arrangements

Market structure and collusion conditions

  • Stigler (1964)
    • topic: a theory of oligopoly (J Political Economy)
    • relevance: identifies structural conditions — number of sellers, product homogeneity, observability, frequency — that determine whether firms can sustain collusion; predicts when procurement corruption takes cartel vs. bilateral form

Incomplete contracts and procurement design

  • Hart, Shleifer, and Vishny (1997)

    • topic: the proper scope of government: theory and an application to prisons (QJE)
    • relevance: incomplete-contracts model where cost-reduction vs. quality-degradation tradeoff depends on contractibility; procurement corruption exploits the residual discretion created by incompleteness

  • Bajari and Tadelis (2001)

    • topic: incentives versus transaction costs: a theory of procurement contracts (RAND J Economics)
    • relevance: models buyer's choice between fixed-price and cost-plus as tradeoff between incentive power and adaptation costs; complex projects → incomplete contracts → room for post-award manipulation

  • Bajari, McMillan, and Tadelis (2009)

    • topic: auctions versus negotiations in procurement: an empirical analysis (J Law, Econ & Organization)
    • relevance: auctions outperform negotiations for simple projects; negotiations preferred for complex projects needing ex post adaptation — empirical confirmation of Bajari-Tadelis (2001)

  • Tirole (1986b)

    • topic: procurement and renegotiation (J Political Economy)
    • relevance: foundational model of how buyer and contractor renegotiate an incomplete procurement contract after cost revelation; renegotiation undermines cost-reduction incentives — the original hold-up problem in procurement

  • Spiller (2008)

    • topic: an institutional theory of public contracts: regulatory implications (NBER WP 14152)
    • relevance: procurement rigidity is a rational response to third-party opportunism (losing bidders, courts, legislators), not red tape; explains why procurement rules are deliberately inflexible

  • Williamson (1979)

    • topic: transaction-cost economics: the governance of contractual relations (J Law & Economics)
    • relevance: asset specificity as key dimension determining governance structures; foundational for understanding why complex procurement contracts are vulnerable to post-award hold-up
