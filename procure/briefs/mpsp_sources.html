MPSP Public Sources for Procurement Corruption Research

MPSP Public Sources for Procurement Corruption Research

Research date: 2026-02-13

Summary

The Ministerio Publico do Estado de Sao Paulo (MPSP) produces substantial publicly available material relevant to understanding municipal procurement corruption in SP state. The most valuable resources for our project are: (1) detailed press releases on GAECO/GEDEC operations that describe fraud mechanics, (2) the SIS public consultation system for civil inquiries, (3) the TAC database, (4) practitioner manuals on investigating patrimonio publico, and (5) aggregate statistics via the CNMP "um retrato" BI dashboard.

Unlike TJSP judicial decisions (which we already scrape in bulk) or CGU audits (which we have as PDFs), MPSP materials are complementary: they describe the investigation side -- how schemes were uncovered, what evidence was gathered, and what the MP's theory of the case was -- before cases reach TJSP for adjudication.

1. MPSP Institutional Website (www.mpsp.mp.br)

1.1 GAECO (Grupo de Atuacao Especial de Combate ao Crime Organizado)

URL: https://www.mpsp.mp.br/grupos-de-atuacao-especial (umbrella page)

GAECO is the primary MPSP unit investigating organized procurement fraud at the municipal level. They conduct named operations with search warrants and arrests, then issue press releases with detailed descriptions of the fraud mechanics.

1.2 GEDEC (Grupo Especial de Delitos Economicos)

URL: https://www.mpsp.mp.br/gedec

Established 2008. Focuses on crimes against economic order: cartel formation, money laundering, and corruption. GEDEC handles the more complex economic cases, often involving state-level entities or large-scale corruption schemes.

Notable cases:

  • Cartel de aquecedores solares: 7 defendants for cartel in CDHU procurement
  • Theatro Municipal corruption scheme: GEDEC-led operation
  • Lavagem de dinheiro conviction (with Crimes de Prefeitos section)
  • Ultrafarma/ICMS fraud (Operation Icaro, Aug 2025)

Relevance: MODERATE. GEDEC cases tend to be higher-profile and often involve state-level entities rather than municipal procurement. But their cartel investigations directly complement CADE enforcement actions.

1.3 Patrimonio Publico Section

URL: https://www.mpsp.mp.br/patrimonio-publico (returns 403 to programmatic fetch)

This is the landing page for MPSP's patrimonio publico defense activities. Based on search results, it covers:

  • Investigations of illegalities involving public property
  • Improbidade administrativa actions
  • Types: nepotism, bidding fraud, diversion of funds, unlawful enrichment, corruption, illegal contracting, misuse of commissioned positions, fiscal responsibility violations

This is the area that prosecutes improbidade cases that end up as TJSP sentenças in our dataset. The page itself appears to be informational rather than a searchable database.

1.4 CAO Civel (Centro de Apoio Operacional Civel)

Referenced in search results as providing operational support to patrimonio publico prosecutors. May publish guidelines, recommendations, or statistics. The 2021 joint recommendation (No. 08/2021) directed MP members to conduct "busca ativa" for improbidade actions filed by co-legitimated entities -- this followed the Lei 14.230/2021 reform making MP the exclusive plaintiff.

Relevance: LOW-MODERATE for direct data, but potentially useful for understanding institutional selection mechanisms that affect our sample.

2. MPSP Transparency Portal and Case Databases

2.1 Portal da Transparencia

URL: https://www.mpsp.mp.br/portal-da-transparencia

The MPSP transparency portal provides institutional information: budget, personnel, organizational structure. It does NOT appear to contain case-level data on improbidade investigations or prosecutions.

Annual Publication: http://www.mpsp.mp.br/portal/page/portal/Portal_da_Transparencia/Publicacao_Anual Annual reports are published here. These may contain aggregate statistics on improbidade case volumes, but the exact content could not be verified (programmatic access blocked).

Relevance: LOW for case-level data. Potentially useful for aggregate statistics to contextualize our TJSP sample.

2.2 SIS Consulta Publica (Public Procedure Consultation System)

URL: https://sismpconsultapublica.mpsp.mp.br/ConsultarDistribuicao

This is the most structured data source from MPSP. It allows public consultation of ongoing civil procedures (inqueritos civis, procedimentos preparatorios).

Features identified from URL parameters and search results:

  • Browse by unit (UA=unit code) and member (Membro=member ID)
  • Filter by ClassificacaoDoTipoProcedimento (1=extrajudicial, etc.)
  • Paginated results (PaginaAtual parameter)
  • Individual procedure detail pages at: https://sismpconsultapublica.mpsp.mp.br/Detalhe/{procedure_number}
  • Data updated daily from the SISMP Integrated system

Example URLs found:

  • https://sismpconsultapublica.mpsp.mp.br/ConsultarDistribuicao/ObterProcedimentosPorUnidade?UA=202&Membro=10041&ClassificacaoDoTipoProcedimento=1&PaginaAtual=8
  • https://sismpconsultapublica.mpsp.mp.br/Detalhe/140291000000120101
  • https://sismpconsultapublica.mpsp.mp.br/Detalhe/140373000124820134
  • https://sismpconsultapublica.mpsp.mp.br/Detalhe/140695000051220198

Scrapability: The URL structure is parametric and predictable. With the right unit codes for patrimonio publico promotorias, it should be possible to enumerate all extrajudicial procedures systematically. However: (a) the MPSP website blocks basic fetches (403), requiring browser automation; (b) the system may have rate limiting; (c) legal/ethical considerations for systematic scraping of prosecutorial data.

Relevance: HIGH. This system could provide a census of MP patrimonio publico investigations, including those that led to improbidade actions in our TJSP sample. It would allow us to understand the investigation-to-prosecution pipeline and identify cases at earlier stages.

2.3 TAC Database (Termos de Ajustamento de Conduta)

URL: https://sismpconsultapublica.mpsp.mp.br/ConsultarProcedimentos/ObterProcedimentosTac

MPSP publishes confirmed TACs (conduct adjustment agreements). These are extrajudicial settlements where the investigated party agrees to specific remedial actions without formal prosecution.

Relevance: MODERATE. TACs represent an alternative resolution path to formal improbidade actions. They may describe procurement irregularities that were resolved without reaching TJSP. Understanding the TAC vs. prosecution decision could inform our discussion of sample selection.

2.4 Processual Consultation (sis.mpsp.mp.br)

URL: https://sis.mpsp.mp.br/consultaprocessual/inqueritos-civis/

A second consultation system focused specifically on civil inquiries and police inquiries. The search results suggest it allows searching for the existence of civil procedures in specific areas including improbidade administrativa.

Relevance: MODERATE. Overlaps with the SIS Consulta Publica but may offer different search capabilities.

3. Diario Oficial do MPSP

URL: https://www.mpsp.mp.br/diario-oficial

The MPSP publishes daily in its own Diario Oficial:

  • Resolutions, ordinances, notices
  • Superior Council decisions
  • Meeting minutes of internal bodies
  • Functional movements (staffing)
  • Instauracoes (opening) and archivamentos (closing) of inqueritos civis
  • Publications related to ongoing proceedings

The state Diario Oficial do Estado de Sao Paulo (https://doe.sp.gov.br/) and the Imprensa Oficial system (https://www.imprensaoficial.com.br/) also publish MPSP actions, with a searchable database that can locate bidding, names, and official acts across all 27 states.

Scrapability: The DO MPSP is accessible via the MPSP website (search by date, text). The Imprensa Oficial has its own search system. Neither appears to offer bulk download. Systematic extraction of inquerito civil instauracoes related to licitacao/improbidade would be possible but labor-intensive.

Relevance: LOW-MODERATE. The DO contains procedural milestones but not case narratives. Useful for building a timeline of investigations but not for understanding scheme mechanics.

6. Notable MPSP Operations: Scheme Typologies Revealed

The GAECO/GEDEC operations above reveal specific procurement corruption typologies that can be cross-referenced with our TJSP case classification:

6.1 PCC-Linked Outsourcing Cartel (Operacao Munditia)

Scheme mechanics:

  • Criminal faction (PCC) controls front companies providing outsourced labor (cleaning, security, maintenance) to municipal governments
  • Companies from the same economic group pose as independent competitors
  • Systematic corruption of municipal officials: secretaries, council presidents, purchasing agents, city councilors
  • Companies held contracts exceeding R$200 million across 13+ municipalities
  • Money laundering through cash, checks (R$3.5M seized), and dollars

Relevance for typology: This represents a organized-crime-driven variant of procurement fraud that goes beyond the typical "local corruption" model. The PCC connection suggests a supply-side driver (criminal organization seeking legitimate revenue streams) rather than purely demand-side (corrupt official seeking rents).

6.2 School Materials Cartel (Operacao Dolos)

Scheme mechanics:

  • Firms specialized in school uniforms and materials form a cartel
  • Corruption of municipal officials to insert directed clauses in editais (specification tailoring / direcionamento)
  • Price-fixing: firms agree to divide lots so all "win" portions
  • Shell companies (empresas de fachada) at the same physical address
  • Nominees (laranjas) as formal owners to hide real controllers
  • Scale: 24 firms, 12 prefeituras, 25 municipalities, R$40M+

Relevance for typology: Classic bid-rigging cartel with corruption overlay. Maps to CADE typology categories: cover bidding, market division, plus specification tailoring (which is a corruption-dependent strategy not in the pure cartel taxonomy).

6.3 Bariri Municipal Racket (Operacao Prenunciado)

Scheme mechanics:

  • Pre-directed bidding through specification manipulation and budget simulation
  • Physical intimidation: military police officers stationed at bidding sessions to threaten and coerce potential competitors from participating
  • Violence and serious threats against potential whistleblowers
  • Kickbacks from contract payment flows
  • Multi-year operation controlling municipal procurement

Relevance for typology: This is an extreme variant where physical coercion replaces economic incentives for maintaining cartel discipline. The involvement of armed police officers as enforcement agents is a distinctive feature that likely leaves different traces in procurement data (very few bidders, no competitive pressure at all).

6.4 Emergency Works Without Bidding (SP Capital Investigation)

Scheme mechanics:

  • Classification of works as "emergenciais" to justify dispensation from competitive bidding (Lei 8.666 Art. 24, IV)
  • "Fabricated emergencies" where the urgency was artificial
  • 303 emergency contracts totaling R$5 billion under the Nunes administration
  • Suspected cartel among contractors receiving no-bid emergency contracts
  • Suspected superfaturamento of R$67 million (per TCM-SP audit)
  • 36 contracts for "emergency" works done without licitacao

Relevance for typology: This represents the dispensacao abuse / false emergency scheme, one of the most common corruption vectors in our TJSP cases (70.9% of cases mention "dispensa"). The SP capital investigation provides a large-scale, well-documented example.
